## 1. Location, period, patron, and how/when each was lost

### Takeaway
Most "lost" libraries were lost slowly (neglect, defunding, earthquakes, conquest) rather than in one fire. Only a few have a single datable catastrophe (Nineveh 612 BCE, Herculaneum 79 CE, Maní 1562, Buda 1526/1541, Nalanda c.1200 CE, Timbuktu 2012-13 threat).

### Cited Findings
- **Alexandria (Mouseion/Great Library)**: founded under Ptolemy I (Mouseion in his reign), Egypt, 3rd c. BCE. Four events are usually blamed: Caesar 48 BCE, Aurelian 3rd c. CE, Theodosius decree 391 CE, Muslim conquest 642 CE; modern historians doubt Caesar's fire destroyed it completely; gradual neglect is a main argument; two sites existed (Mouseion library and the Serapeum daughter) and they were likely lost at different times — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Library-of-Alexandria/The-fate-of-the-Library-of-Alexandria), [OpenLearn](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/library-alexandria/content-section-3), [Bad Ancient](https://www.badancient.com/claims/julius-caesar-burn-library-of-alexandria/), [qcurtius](https://qcurtius.com/2019/04/14/the-alexandrian-library-dissipation-through-neglect-and-apathy/). Contemporary accounts say Caesar's men started a fire that destroyed buildings near the harbor in 48 BCE (same sources).
- **Serapeum**: temple of Serapis built by Ptolemy III Euergetes (246-222 BCE), housed an offshoot collection; Pompey's Pillar (Diocletian, c.298-302 CE) is the main surviving feature; destroyed 391 CE after Theodosius I's ban on pagan worship; sources disagree whether it was a pagan defensive stand or a Christian assault; later a church/monastery of St John the Baptist, then a Muslim cemetery; excavations in 1944 found foundation deposits (plaques, coins, marble fragments) — [Wikipedia: Serapeum of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serapeum_of_Alexandria).
- **Ashurbanipal, Nineveh**: Assyrian king's palace library, Kouyunjik mound; found from 1849 (Layard, ~20,000 tablets reported); most now at the British Museum, whose database counts 30,943 "tablets" for the Nineveh library collection — [Wikipedia (mirror)](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Ashurbanipal), [CJS](https://cojs.org/royal_library_of_ashurbanipal-_7th_century_bce/). Fall of Nineveh 612 BCE **[bg]**; the fire that burned the palace baked tablets and preserved them (irony worth telling) **[bg]**.
- **Pergamon**: built by Eumenes II (reign 197-159 BCE; source says 220-159), Bergama, Turkey; ~200,000 volumes claimed (ancient claim, unreliable); Antony-gives-it-to-Cleopatra story is a legend; earthquake 262 CE and Gothic attacks; German excavations 1885-1890 — [Wikipedia: Library of Pergamum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Pergamum). Parchment "invented at Pergamon" is a myth (same source).
- **Celsus, Ephesus**: interior destroyed 262 CE (earthquake or Gothic invasion), facade toppled by an earthquake in the 10th-11th c.; facade rebuilt by anastylosis, campaign led by Volker M. Strocka 1970-78 — [Reading Virtual Rome](https://research.reading.ac.uk/virtualrome/2013/08/19/ancient-libraries-2/), [Brewminate](https://brewminate.com/the-library-of-celsus-in-ancient-ephesus/), [Duke page](https://people.duke.edu/~wj25/uc_web_site/libraries/celsus.html). Built c.110-135 CE as tomb-monument to Tiberius Julius Celsus Polemaeanus by his son Aquila **[bg]**.
- **Nalanda**: Bihar, India, 5th c. CE to c.1200 CE **[bg]**. Dharmaganja library = three buildings Ratnasagara (9 storeys), Ratnodadhi, Ratnaranjaka per later Tibetan tradition; Taranatha's descriptions are vague and do not locate Dharmaganja on the campus — [Organiser summary](https://organiser.org/2025/12/13/329939/bharat/ancient-wisdom-of-nalanda-mahavihara-exploring-the-legendary-library-and-its-global-scholarly-influence/amp/) (weak, partisan source). Destruction by Bakhtiyar Khilji c.1190s-1200s is from Minhaj-i-Siraj's Tabaqat-i-Nasiri, which does not clearly name Nalanda; the "library burned for 3 months" is a late embellishment; the complex also declined before **[bg]**.
- **Timbuktu**: 14th-20th c. manuscript culture. During 2012 al-Qaeda occupation, staff and families smuggled tens of thousands of manuscripts south to Bamako — [Africanews 2025](https://www.africanews.com/2025/11/16/timbuktus-ancient-manuscripts-return-after-years-away/). Ahmed Baba Institute returned ~28,000 manuscripts to Timbuktu after digitisation (reported Aug 2025); private libraries hold an estimated 377,000 in total (remaining in Bamako per one summary) — [Africanews 19 Nov 2025](https://www.africanews.com/amp/2025/11/19/timbuktus-ancient-manuscripts-return-home-but-security-threats-remain/), [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/world/archives/2025/11/18/2003847412). The 2013 library fire at Ahmed Baba (jihadists burned part of the collection, ~4,000 of ~40,000 held there; most manuscripts had been hidden) **[bg]**; the 100%-saved claim of "350,000" is disputed **[bg]**.
- **Villa of the Papyri**: Herculaneum, buried by Vesuvius 79 CE; found 1750 (well-digging), Karl Weber tunnels 1750-65; excavations resumed 1980s-90s revealing lower floors; "over 1,800" scrolls, carbonised; possibly assembled by Philodemus of Gadara; Getty Villa is a replica — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_of_the_Papyri). Owner likely L. Calpurnius Piso Caesoninus **[bg]**.
- **Bibliotheca Corviniana**: Matthias Corvinus, Buda, from c.1460 to 1490; ~3,000 codices; after the Ottoman conquest moved to Istanbul; ~216 Corvinae survive; UNESCO Memory of the World items 2005; National Széchényi Library digitising — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliotheca_Corviniana). Buda fell 1541; Mohács 1526; much dispersal was before 1526 (Vladislaus II neglect) and the Ottoman plunder of Buda 1526 was partial **[bg]**. Some returned by Sultan Abdul Hamid II gifts in 1869 and 1877 **[bg]**.
- **Dunhuang Cave 17**: Mogao Caves, sealed c.early 11th c., rediscovered c.1900 by Wang Yuanlu; Stein 1907, Pelliot 1908 removed large parts; manuscripts now split among British Library, BnF, St Petersburg, Beijing, Japan, etc. — majority of surviving texts date late 4th-early 11th c. and come from Cave 17 per [Wikipedia: Dunhuang manuscripts](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunhuang_manuscript). Cave 17 was a "library cave" is the standard label but whether it was a sealed library, a repository for worn-out manuscripts (genizah-like), or a hasty hiding place is debated **[bg]**; reason for sealing (Xi Xia threat vs. ritual burial) is unknown **[bg]**.
- **Maya codices / Maní 1562**: Bishop Diego de Landa's auto-da-fé at Maní, Yucatán, July 1562, destroyed "27 rolls" and thousands of images per Landa's own Relación **[bg]**. Only 4 (or 3 certain plus the Grolier, authenticated 2016) pre-Columbian codices survive: Dresden, Madrid (Tro-Cortesianus), Paris, Grolier **[bg]**.
- **Library of Hadrian (Athens)**, **Ulpian Library (Rome)**, **Imperial Library of Constantinople**, **Córdoba (al-Hakam II)**, **House of Wisdom**, **Hanlin/Siku**, **Ebla**, **Hattusa**: see per-library sections below; sourcing mostly **[bg]** because searches budget ran out.

### Inferences
- Honest storytelling frame: "slow loss" libraries (Alexandria, Pergamon, Constantinople, Baghdad) vs "catastrophe" libraries (Nineveh, Herculaneum, Maní) vs "dispersal" (Dunhuang, Corviniana) vs "rescued" (Timbuktu).

### Gaps
- No primary excavation reports were fetched; dates for Eumenes II conflict (220 vs 197 BCE); verify.

## 2. Library-by-library: known vs speculative, physical evidence, architecture

### Takeaway
Only Celsus (facade), Pergamon (foundations), Villa of the Papyri (villa plan + scrolls), Nineveh (tablets, palace relief plans), Ebla/Hattusa (excavated archive rooms) and Dunhuang (cave itself) give hard architecture. Alexandria, House of Wisdom, Dharmaganja, Constantinople and Córdoba are mostly text-based and must be shown as conjectural.

### Cited Findings / Notes by library

**Alexandria (Mouseion library)**
- KNOWN: it existed; royal patronage; scholar-librarians (Zenodotus, Callimachus' Pinakes catalogue, Eratosthenes, Aristarchus); copying of books from ships (Galen's report) **[bg]**. Strabo describes the Mouseion in the royal quarter (Brucheion) with a peripatos, exedra and a large dining hall **[bg]**.
- UNKNOWN: location of the actual book rooms; size (ancient figures of 40,000 to 700,000 scrolls are unreliable); no identified ruins. Polish-Egyptian excavations at Kom el-Dikka found "auditoria" (lecture halls, 5th-7th c. CE) — often misread as the library, but they are late Roman, not Ptolemaic **[bg]**. Underwater work in the Eastern Harbour (Goddio, Empereur) found royal quarter remains **[bg]**.
- Reconstruction approach: speculative. Show a peristyle/stoa with colonnaded reading walks and book storage in side rooms; flag all as conjecture. Papyrus roll storage: scrolls in pigeonholes/bins with tags (sillyboi) **[bg]**.
- Modern Bibliotheca Alexandrina (2002) is a living library, not in scope.

**Serapeum**
- KNOWN: location on the Rhakotis hill; Pompey's Pillar; underground galleries and Apis-bull galleries; foundation plaques (Ptolemy III) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serapeum_of_Alexandria). Rooms cut into the rock sometimes called library niches: the niches in the colonnade (Rowe/Botti excavations) are suggestive but the library identification rests on Aphthonius' 4th c. description **[bg]**.
- SPECULATIVE: scroll count (Epiphanius says 42,800 **[bg]**), the fittings.

**Ashurbanipal (Nineveh)**
- KNOWN: tablets (library + archive), colophons ("Palace of Ashurbanipal, king of the world, king of Assyria"), tablet ownership curse formulae **[bg]**. Found in North Palace and Southwest Palace, Kouyunjik **[bg]**. Layard 1849-51, Rassam 1852-54 and later; Rassam found the North Palace library **[bg]**.
- UNKNOWN: shelving. Tablets were found fallen, mixed; no intact shelves; original rooms had probably wooden shelves/baskets/boxes with clay labels ("docket" tags) **[bg]**; heights and room arrangement are reconstructions.
- Digital: British Museum imaging since 2002 with University of Mosul; images (14 photos per tablet) on CDLI and BM collection online — [Wikipedia mirror](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Ashurbanipal). Machine-learning joins of fragments to reconstruct Gilgamesh passages — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/ai-is-helping-us-read-ancient-mesopotamian-literature-205091). Gilgamesh XI flood tablet (BM K.3375) found by George Smith 1872 **[bg]**; BM object record e.g. [W K 3382](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_K-3382).
- Palace relief panels (lamassu, lion hunt) are real visual references for the palace; the library was in rooms off the palace **[bg]**.

**House of Wisdom (Bayt al-Hikma), Baghdad**
- KNOWN: Abbasid caliphs (Harun al-Rashid, al-Ma'mun) 8th-9th c.; translation movement (Hunayn ibn Ishaq, al-Khwarizmi, Banu Musa) **[bg]**; destroyed or declined by the Mongol sack of 1258 (Tigris "ran black with ink" is a late, poorly attested story) **[bg]**.
- UNKNOWN: building, location, even whether it was a distinct institution vs. a caliphal library; Dimitri Gutas and others argue "House of Wisdom" was a Sasanian-style book repository, not a university. No ruins, no plans. Round Baghdad (al-Mansur) is archaeologically lost under the modern city **[bg]**.
- Reconstruction: purely imaginative; use Abbasid-era architecture (Samarra, Ukhaidir palace) as analogue and label as such.

**Nalanda / Dharmaganja**
- KNOWN: excavated monastery ruins (ASI, Hiralal Hindu/Sarkar excavations 1915-37, Ghosh), UNESCO World Heritage 2016 **[bg]**; Xuanzang (7th c.) and Yijing accounts describe halls, library, 10,000 monks **[bg]**.
- UNKNOWN: location and plan of Dharmaganja; the "nine-storey" claim is from Tibetan sources written centuries later — [Organiser](https://organiser.org/2025/12/13/329939/bharat/ancient-wisdom-of-nalanda-mahavihara-exploring-the-legendary-library-and-its-global-scholarly-influence/amp/); Taranatha's descriptions "don't mention the exact location". Temple 3 (stupa) and Monastery 1 are the best visible structures **[bg]**.
- Reconstruction: generate a brick-built multi-storey stepped hall as a flagged hypothesis; manuscripts = palm-leaf pothi in wooden covers, in cloth wraps on shelves **[bg]**.

**Pergamon**
- KNOWN: Athena precinct, library rooms on north side of the Athena sanctuary stoa; four rooms; main hall ~44.5 x 50 ft (reported in [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Pergamum)); 50 cm gap between outer wall and shelving for ventilation; a ~3 m Athena statue in the main room (copy of Phidias' Parthenos found in Berlin) **[bg]**. Foundation/podium for wooden shelving survives; shelf supports of wood and stone podium visible **[bg]**. Interpretation of the rooms as a library has been questioned by some (the "library" may partly have been a royal reception hall) **[bg]**.
- Pergamon Museum Berlin holds the Pergamon Altar; Pergamon Museum's north wing is closed for renovation to 2027 or later **[bg]**.

**Celsus (Ephesus)**
- KNOWN: two-storey Corinthian facade (anastylosis, 1970-78) — [Reading Virtual Rome](https://research.reading.ac.uk/virtualrome/2013/08/19/ancient-libraries-2/); the interior is mostly a rectangular room ~ 17 x 21 m with niches on three levels, a central apse with the sarcophagus of Celsus in a crypt below, double wall for ventilation **[bg]** (check dims). Facade statues: Sophia, Arete, Ennoia, Episteme (the copies in situ; originals in the Ephesus Museum, Vienna) **[bg]**. Library capacity est. 12,000 scrolls **[bg]**.
- SPECULATIVE: ceiling (flat timber vs. barrel-vault), the exact fittings of the niches, lighting (clerestory from windows high up).
- Best-evidenced candidate in the set.

**Timbuktu**
- KNOWN: private family libraries (Mamma Haidara, Kati, Imam Essayouti, Fondo Kati) in mud-brick and timber houses; manuscripts stored in metal trunks and wooden boxes by the 2012 crisis; Ahmed Baba Institute (IHERI-AB) with the new South African-funded building (2009, Mamma Haidara library ones too) **[bg]**. Material: Arabic, Songhay, Tamasheq, Fula texts; gold-leaf illuminated Qur'ans **[bg]**. Collection status: [Africanews](https://www.africanews.com/2025/11/16/timbuktus-ancient-manuscripts-return-after-years-away/).
- Digitized: Tombouctou Manuscripts Project, UCT — [site](https://tombouctoumanuscripts.uct.ac.za/); Hill Museum & Manuscript Library (HMML) digitisation; SAVAMA-DCI **[bg]**.
- "Library" is a distributed network; best presented as a walk through a family house, not a single building.

**Villa of the Papyri**
- KNOWN: sea-front villa, ~250 m long, peristyle garden with long pool (the Getty replica follows the plan), tablinum, a small room with shelves where scrolls were found (probably a library) plus scrolls in other rooms/boxes (some found in wooden cases) **[bg]**. Scrolls: ~1,800 recovered ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_of_the_Papyri)); philosophical (Epicurean), mostly Philodemus. Only a fraction of the villa has been excavated; two lower floors found in 1990s; further excavation proposed.
- Holdings: Officina dei Papiri Ercolanesi, Biblioteca Nazionale di Napoli (most scrolls); a few in Oxford (Bodleian), Paris (Institut de France), etc. **[bg]**.

**Vesuvius Challenge (latest)**
- 2023: first words (πορφύρας, "purple") read; Grand Prize Feb 2024 (~2,000 chars, Philodemus on pleasure) **[bg]**.
- June 25, 2026: PHerc. 1667 read end to end without unrolling: ~1.5 m of continuous text, about 20 columns; identified as Philodemus, On Gods, Book 8 (reads Φ̣ι[λοδ]ή̣μου | περὶ θεῶν Η̅) — via ESRF EBS beamline BM18 scan data; prize total >$1.8M; ~300 intact scrolls remain sealed — search summaries from [AI Reads Herculaneum's Sealed Scroll](https://creativesunite.eu/article/ai-reads-herculaneum-s-sealed-scroll-for-the-first-time), [ESRF news](https://www.esrf.fr/home/news/general/content-news/general/the-day-the-herculaneum-scrolls-began-speaking-again.html), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/a-volcano-toasted-this-scroll-2000-years-ago-heres-how-scientists-read-it-without-ever-opening-it-2000803940), [Tribune 15 Jul 2026](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2615079/researchers-use-ai-to-unlock-the-complete-text-of-burnt-herculaneum-scroll-for-the-first-time). Search snippets were aggregator-level; the Diamond Light Source 2026 release returned HTTP 403 (not read). Another headline (Timeline Auctions summary) says two scrolls read at last, taking opposite sides on whether gods run the world — unverified.
- A Stoic text identification in one summary ("Stoic work on human nature, impulse") conflicts with the Philodemus (Epicurean) attribution; the content description is from a low-quality aggregator, treat as unreliable.

**Hadrian's Library (Athens)** **[bg]**
- AD 132; rectangular enclosure ~122 x 82 m, peristyle courtyard with 100 columns of Phrygian marble, pool, west propylon (Corinthian columns survive), rooms on the east side (library, lecture halls, reading rooms with niches); excavated and consolidated by Greek archaeologists; site in Monastiraki. Ruins above ground are real; Pausanias describes it. Strong candidate for a layout-based reconstruction. Destroyed by Herulian raid 267 CE; later churches built within.

**Ulpian Library (Rome)** **[bg]**
- Trajan's Forum, dedicated c.112-113 CE: two halls (Greek and Latin) flanking Trajan's Column in a courtyard. Excavated portions; Column survives; Marble Plan (Forma Urbis) fragments; two-storey niches with podium and columns; architect Apollodorus of Damascus; later references to Ulpia library into the 5th c.; fate unclear. Trajan's Column's own library function is debated (the stairs hall). Restoration studies (Meneghini) exist.

**Imperial Library of Constantinople** **[bg]**
- Founded by Constantius II 357 CE; reorganised by Julian and Theodosius; reached ~120,000 volumes; a fire in 475 destroyed the main library (the "serpent's gut" Homer on dragon-skin legend); partial destruction in 726, the Fourth Crusade 1204, and the Ottoman conquest 1453 destroyed much; Patriarchal library persisted. Location uncertain (near the Basilica or the Portico of Achilles). No plans.

**Córdoba (al-Hakam II)** **[bg]**
- Caliph al-Hakam II (r. 961-976); claims of 400,000 volumes with a 44-volume catalogue (from Ibn Hazm/al-Humaydi, probably exaggerated); the library was dispersed or destroyed by al-Mansur's purge c.981 (to appease clerics) and in the fitna of 1010. Location probably in Madinat al-Zahra' or the Alcázar; no remains identified. Madinat al-Zahra' excavated; UNESCO 2018; the Salón Rico is the visual reference.

**Hanlin Academy & Siku Quanshu** **[bg]**
- Siku Quanshu (1773-1782, Qianlong); 36,000+ volumes (~79,000 juan); 7 sets copied for 7 pavilions: Wenyuan Ge (Forbidden City, 1776, still standing and restored; the pavilion uses black tile and water motifs against fire; two-storey exterior, six internal), Wenyuan (Wenjin Ge, Chengde, extant), Wenyuan Ge, Wenlan Ge (Hangzhou, partly lost; set rebuilt from the Ding family copies), Wenzong Ge (Zhenjiang, destroyed 1853), Wenhui Ge (Yangzhou, destroyed 1853), Wensu Ge (Shenyang, extant). The Wenyuan set went to Taiwan 1948 (National Palace Museum). Wenyuan Ge: still extant; the "lost" aspect: Hanlin Academy library (Beijing) burned in 1900 during the Boxer siege, destroying the Yongle Encyclopedia's remaining volumes (only ~400 of 11,095 survive) **[bg]**. The Hanlin Academy fire of June 1900 is a real, dated event. Pavilions are the best-evidenced architecture; the Hanlin site has been rebuilt as a different complex.

**Maya codices** **[bg]**
- Codices were folded amate/bark-paper screenfolds with lime coating, not scrolls; kept by priests in temples or elite houses. No library building identified; the "library" is conceptual. Surviving: Dresden (Saxon State Library, SLUB, high-res free digital copy) , Madrid (Museo de América), Paris (BnF), Grolier (Mexico). Diego de Landa regretted, then used the Relación to record the Maya script syllabary. Modern Maya in Yucatán continue to use the books' knowledge. Do NOT show a "library" of shelves.

**Ebla & Hattusa** **[bg]**
- Ebla (Tell Mardikh, Syria): c.2500-2250 BCE archive, ~17,000 tablets found 1974-76 by Paolo Matthiae in Palace G, Room L.2769, burned with the palace c.2300 (Akkadian destruction, debated), the tablets had been on wooden shelves that collapsed: archaeologists could reconstruct the original shelf order from the fall pattern. Held in Idlib and Aleppo museums; Syrian war endangered the site.
- Hattusa (Boğazkale, Turkey): Hittite capital; ~30,000 tablets (from 1906 Winckler); archive rooms in Büyükkale citadel and the Temple 1 magazines; tablets in Hittite, Akkadian, Hurrian and others; includes the Treaty of Kadesh (Egyptian-Hittite) copy; Istanbul Archaeology Museums and Ankara. The Hattusa site is a UNESCO site; partial reconstructions of the walls exist (the Hattusa Hittite wall reconstruction).

### Inferences
- Rank by hard geometry: Celsus > Hadrian > Pergamon > Villa of the Papyri > Ebla > Hattusa > Nineveh > Siku pavilions > Nalanda > Alexandria/Serapeum > Constantinople > Córdoba > House of Wisdom.

### Gaps
- Dimensions marked [bg] need verifying against excavation reports (e.g., Ephesus: Österreichisches Archäologisches Institut; Athens: Hadrian's Library, Greek Ministry of Culture; Rome: Packer's Forum of Trajan).
- No source retrieved for Hanlin/Siku, Constantinople, Córdoba, Ebla/Hattusa details.

## 3. Storage, lighting, materials (for artists)

### Cited Findings
- Greek/Roman: papyrus rolls in wall niches (armaria) with wooden cabinet doors; the niche wall set back from the outer wall (Pergamon 50 cm gap; Celsus double wall) to reduce humidity — [Wikipedia Pergamum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Pergamum), [Reading Virtual Rome](https://research.reading.ac.uk/virtualrome/2013/08/19/ancient-libraries-2/). Labels on rolls (sillybos tags) **[bg]**.
- Mesopotamia: tablets on shelves or in baskets/boxes; clay dockets; colophons **[bg]**.
- Herculaneum: scrolls in a small room with shelves and also in cases; they look like charcoal logs, ~ 2-30 cm wide when found **[bg]**.
- Timbuktu: manuscripts in trunks/boxes; leather satchels; mud brick buildings — [Africanews](https://www.africanews.com/2025/11/16/timbuktus-ancient-manuscripts-return-after-years-away/) (partial).
- Lighting: natural light through high windows/doorways; lamps avoided in book rooms for fire risk **[bg]**.

### Gaps
- No single source for lighting; treat as interpretive.

## 6. Myth vs fact (do not repeat)

### Cited Findings
- "Caesar burned the Great Library in one fire": modern historians doubt the library was entirely destroyed — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Library-of-Alexandria/The-fate-of-the-Library-of-Alexandria), [Bad Ancient](https://www.badancient.com/claims/julius-caesar-burn-library-of-alexandria/).
- "Omar burned it in 642": late (12th c.) tradition **[bg]**; same four-event list.
- "Parchment was invented at Pergamon": false — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Pergamum). "Antony gave Pergamon's books to Cleopatra": legend (same).
- "Nalanda burned for three months": from late Tibetan/Persian embellishment **[bg]**; Dharmaganja layout unknown (see above).
- "Whole Villa of the Papyri library was read": no; ~1,800 recovered, ~300 intact remain sealed.
- "Ink of Baghdad's rivers ran black": poorly attested **[bg]**.
- "Timbuktu: 700,000 manuscripts": figures vary widely (377,000 estimate for private libraries in [Macau Daily Times summary](https://macaudailytimes.com.mo/timbuktus-famed-manuscripts-escaped-al-qaida-but-the-threat-remains.html)); "all saved" is overstated; the 2013 burning was real but partial **[bg]**.
- "Maya books were all destroyed": 4 survive; Maya oral and inscribed tradition lives on.
- "Cave 17 was a library": convention; purpose debated **[bg]**.

## 7. Additional libraries worth adding (with reasons) **[bg]**
- **Library of Pantainos (Athens Agora, c.100 CE)**: only inscription-attested rules ("no book shall be taken out; open from 1st to 6th hour"), real plan in Agora excavations; great for a small intimate mock-up and for rules/humor.
- **Sankore (Timbuktu)**: mosque-university, partly standing; overlap with Timbuktu candidate.
- **Qumran / Dead Sea Scrolls**: caves 1-11 (found 1947-56), ~900 scrolls; whether Qumran had a "library" vs scrolls hidden from Rome (c.68 CE) is debated; Israel Museum Shrine of the Book and digital project (Leon Levy); 3D cave scans possible.
- **Monte Cassino (c.529 onward)**: Benedictine; destroyed by Allied bombing Feb 1944 (manuscripts evacuated by the Wehrmacht to Rome beforehand); rebuilt; archive survives; medieval armarium and scriptorium.
- **Others**: Library of Hypatia/Serapeum (see above); Sassanid Gundeshapur; Great Mosque Library of Kairouan; Abbey of Bobbio / Vivarium (Cassiodorus); Library of Alexandria's "Daughter" at Caesareum; Ban Zhao's Dongguan Han library; Bodleian's Duke Humfrey's (still in use, so out of scope).
