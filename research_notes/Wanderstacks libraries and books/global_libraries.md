# Non-European iconic libraries as 3D replica candidates (Wanderstacks)

Scope note: research session of ~25 tool calls (Wikipedia pages, a few web searches). Official sites mostly not fetched (loc.gov returned 403). Anything not cited below is a GAP, not a fact. Numbers marked "conflict" differ between sources. Date of research: Nov 2026.

## 1. Location, founding, construction dates, architects, style

### Takeaway
Core spaces are mostly Beaux-Arts/Renaissance Revival (1895-1911) or 21st-century architect-icons. Dates and architects verified for most; Morgan, Bates Hall, Qarawiyyin architecture details thin.

### Cited Findings
- **LoC Thomas Jefferson Building Main Reading Room (Washington DC)**: opened to public 1 Nov 1897 (finishing 1898). Architects: Smithmeyer (dismissed 1888), Paul J. Pelz (main designer), Edward Pearce Casey (took over 1892, interiors). 50+ American painters/sculptors contributed. — [Wikipedia: Thomas Jefferson Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_Building)
- **NYPL Stephen A. Schwarzman Building, Rose Main Reading Room (NYC)**: Carrere & Hastings, Beaux-Arts, built 1900-1911; reading room closed July 1997, reopened 16 Nov 1998 as Rose Main Reading Room after $15M gift. — [Wikipedia: Stephen A. Schwarzman Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_A._Schwarzman_Building); [NYC LPC / search summary](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/lpc/downloads/pdf/proposed_landmarks/NYPL%20Interiors%20blurb%206-6-17.pdf)
- **George Peabody Library (Baltimore)**: funded 1857, opened 1878; architect Edmund G. Lind with provost Nathaniel Morison; to Johns Hopkins 1982; renovated 2004 ($1M); city landmark 1975. — [Wikipedia: George Peabody Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peabody_Library)
- **Real Gabinete Portugues de Leitura (Rio)**: society founded 14 May 1837; building 1880-1887; architect Rafael da Silva e Castro; Neo-Manueline (after Jeronimos Monastery); facade carved of Lisbon stone by Germano Jose Salle; open to public since 1900. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Gabinete_Portugu%C3%AAs_de_Leitura)
- **Boston Public Library Bates Hall**: McKim Building by Charles Follen McKim (McKim, Mead & White), completed 1895, Renaissance style modelled on Bibliotheque Sainte-Genevieve (Paris); NHL 1986. Hall named for benefactor Joshua Bates. — [Wikipedia: Boston Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Public_Library); [BPL guide](https://guides.bpl.org/mckim/bateshall)
- **Morgan Library (NYC)**: McKim, Mead & White (Charles McKim), built 1902-1906, Morgan used from Nov 1906, decoration done Jan 1907, cost ~$1.2M; opened to public Dec 1942; Renzo Piano expansion 2003-2006 reopened 29 Apr 2006 ($106M). — [Wikipedia: Morgan Library & Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Library_%26_Museum)
- **Beinecke (Yale)**: Gordon Bunshaft, SOM, completed 1963; modernist; closed ~18 months for 2015-16 renovation. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beinecke_Rare_Book_%26_Manuscript_Library)
- **Library of Parliament (Ottawa)**: established 1876 (library collection older); architects Thomas Fuller and Chilion Jones; High Victorian Gothic, circular chapter-house plan; survived 1916 fire (iron doors closed); 1952 fire separate. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Parliament)
- **State Library Victoria La Trobe Reading Room (Melbourne)**: library founded 1854 as Melbourne Public Library; dome opened 1913, designed by Norman G. Peebles of Bates Smart (successor firm to Joseph Reed). Wikipedia also states "largest dome in the world on completion" (claim to treat cautiously). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Library_Victoria)
- **Tianjin Binhai Library**: MVRDV with Tianjin Urban Planning and Design Institute; opened Oct 2017 (~3 years from preliminary design). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianjin_Binhai_Library)
- **Bibliotheca Alexandrina**: Snohetta won 1988 competition; opened 16 Oct 2002. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliotheca_Alexandrina)
- **Qatar National Library (Doha)**: OMA / Rem Koolhaas; soft opening Nov 2017, inaugurated 16 Apr 2018. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Library)
- **Seattle Central Library**: OMA/LMN (Koolhaas, Joshua Prince-Ramus); opened 23 May 2004. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Central_Library)
- **Biblioteca Vasconcelos (Mexico City)**: Alberto Kalach and Juan Palomar; opened 16 May 2006; closed Mar 2007 for construction defects, reopened Nov 2008. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Vasconcelos)
- **El Ateneo Grand Splendid (Buenos Aires) - A BOOKSTORE in a former THEATRE, not a library**: Teatro Gran Splendid opened May 1919 (architects Pero and Torres Armengol, 1,050 seats), cinema from late 1920s (first sound films in Argentina, 1929), converted to bookstore ~2000 (architect Fernando Manzone, ~2,000 m2); ceiling frescoes Nazareno Orlandi, caryatids Troiano Troiani; seating in old boxes, cafe on former stage. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ateneo_Grand_Splendid)
- **National Library of China (Beijing)**: Phase II 1987; Phase III (2008) by KSP Jurgen Engel; Ancient Books Hall elsewhere (Xicheng); ~280,000 m2 total. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_China)
- **Rampur Raza Library (UP, India)**: founded 1774 by Nawab Faizullah Khan; moved 1957 to Hamid Manzil (early-20th-c. palace). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampur_Raza_Library)
- **Khuda Bakhsh Oriental Library (Patna)**: founded 29 Oct 1891 by Khuda Bakhsh Khan (4,000 mss start); national-importance status 1969. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuda_Bakhsh_Oriental_Library)
- **Kanazawa Umimirai Library (Japan)**: Kazumi Kudo + Hiroshi Horiba (Coelacanth K&H), completed Mar 2011; JIA Prize 2013. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanazawa_Umimirai_Library)
- **Al-Qarawiyyin Library (Fez)**: founded 859 (per search summaries); 2012 Ministry of Culture commissioned Aziza Chaouni to rehabilitate; reopened to public May 2016. — [Search summary incl. Atlas Obscura / Morocco World News](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/the-worlds-oldest-library-has-reopened.amp); [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2016/03/111376/library-of-al-qarawiyyin-university-in-fez-to-reopen-to-the-public-in-may/)
- **Added candidate: Biblioteca Palafoxiana (Puebla, Mexico)**: founded 1646 when Bishop Juan de Palafox donated ~5,000 volumes; same building since 1646; UNESCO Memory of the World 2005; 45,000+ books/manuscripts; oldest text Nuremberg Chronicle 1493. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Palafoxiana); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/are-origins-oldest-public-library-americas-rcna51478)

### Inferences
- LoC architect credit is contested between Smithmeyer/Pelz/Casey; use "Smithmeyer & Pelz, completed by Casey" in descriptions.

### Gaps
- Exact construction dates for Qarawiyyin's current reading hall (building Marinid-era, 14th c., per my background knowledge, unsourced here) and Khuda Bakhsh building style/date not retrieved.
- Rampur Hamid Manzil architect (W.C. Wright, Indo-Saracenic) is from my memory, NOT verified here.
- Bates Hall opening "1895" confirmed; SLV "Norman Hitchcock" name in brief is wrong per source (Peebles).

## 2. Visual signature (layout, dimensions, materials, art, lighting, palette)

### Takeaway
Numeric data solid for NYPL, Bates, SLV, Alexandria, Peabody, Kanazawa, Beinecke ratio; thin for LoC interior, Gabinete, Morgan, Parliament, Qarawiyyin. Pull HABS drawings for US sites to fill gaps.

### Cited Findings
- **LoC Main Reading Room**: circular; eight large marble columns each topped with a plaster statue (Religion, Commerce, History, Art, Philosophy, Poetry, Law, Science) with bronze portrait statues below; dome with 23-carat gold leaf and Edwin Blashfield's 1896 mural "The Evolution of Civilization" (12 winged figures). Dome height conflict: Wikipedia says building dome 195 ft (exterior/overall); another source says 160 ft above the reading room floor. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson_Building); [Alamy caption (low-quality)](https://www.alamy.de/); [search results](https://www.timeout.com/washington-dc/attractions/library-of-congress)
- **NYPL Rose Main Reading Room**: 297 ft x 78 ft, 52 ft ceiling; one of the largest US rooms without dome, interior columns or steel-reinforced walls; round-arched windows; Carrere & Hastings designed furniture, lighting, hardware; ceiling rosettes restored and cable-supported after one fell in 2014 (closed to 2016/18 repairs - see Q7). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_A._Schwarzman_Building); [NYC LPC](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/lpc/downloads/pdf/proposed_landmarks/NYPL%20Interiors%20blurb%206-6-17.pdf)
- **Peabody**: atrium 61 ft to latticed skylight; five tiers of ornamental cast-iron balconies; 300,000 volumes. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peabody_Library)
- **Gabinete**: tall narrow reading room, iron-structure skylight (first of its type in Brazil) and chandelier; neo-Manueline facade, four statues (Cabral, Camoes, Henry the Navigator, da Gama), medallions (Fernao Lopes, Gil Vicente, Herculano, Garrett), 1.7 m "Altar of the Homeland" silver/ivory/marble. Interior painted 1876-77 per one source (date inconsistent with 1880-87 build). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Gabinete_Portugu%C3%AAs_de_Leitura); [search results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Portuguese_Cabinet_of_Reading)
- **Bates Hall**: 218 ft long, 42 ft wide, 50 ft high; barrel vault (reported as longest in North America); English oak bookcases and tables; two green lamps per table. — [BPL guide](https://guides.bpl.org/mckim/bateshall); [Lost New England](https://lostnewengland.com/?p=5645) (search summary; I did not open full page)
- **Morgan East Room**: three tiers of inlaid walnut bookcases (two tiers added late in construction), concealed staircases behind bookcases, Henry Siddons Mowbray ceiling paintings with zodiac in hexagonal spandrels, 16th-c. Netherlandish Midas/Avarice tapestry over mantel, Gutenberg Bible always on view. McKim Building is white marble, classical. — [Morgan: East Room](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/Pierpont-Morgans-1906-Library/eastroom); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Library_%26_Museum)
- **Beinecke**: height:width:length 1:2:3; Danby (Vermont) marble panels 1.25 in (32 mm) thick admitting amber light; six-story glass book tower in centre; four corner piers; tower holds 180,000 volumes, >1M in underground stacks. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beinecke_Rare_Book_%26_Manuscript_Library)
- **Library of Parliament**: circular chapter-house plan, 16 flying buttresses, three-tier roof with cupola, white pine paneling carved with textures and mythical creatures, Marshall Wood marble Queen Victoria statue. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Parliament)
- **SLV La Trobe**: octagonal; dome 34.75 m diameter and height, oculus ~5 m; originally for 1M books/600 readers, now 32,000 books/320 readers; skylights uncovered 2003 (covered since 1959). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Library_Victoria)
- **Tianjin Binhai**: 33,700 m2, five levels, terraced floor-to-ceiling shelves, central sphere auditorium (110 seats) seen through eye-shaped opening, 1.2M-volume capacity; book spines printed on shelf backs for opening photos (shelves partly dummy at opening). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianjin_Binhai_Library)
- **Alexandria**: tilted glass roof 32 m high, ~160 m diameter; granite wall carved with 120 scripts' characters; reading room 20,000 m2 over 11 cascading levels; 8M-book capacity. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliotheca_Alexandrina)
- **Qatar NL**: details in source limited to programme; building is single large open hall (visual detail from my memory, not verified). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Library)
- **Seattle Central**: 11 stories, 60 m (196 ft), 362,987 sq ft, steel-and-glass diamond grid; Books Spiral on floors 6-9, max 2-degree slope; Living Room (3rd floor); Red Floor (4th). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Central_Library)
- **Vasconcelos**: 38,000 m2; hollowed interior with suspended expandable shelves; Gabriel Orozco's whale skeleton "Ballena". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Vasconcelos)
- **Kanazawa Umimirai**: 45 m x 45 m x 12 m high space; ~6,000 round glass blocks in concrete walls; floor area 5,641.9 m2; 228,000 items. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanazawa_Umimirai_Library)
- **El Ateneo**: see Q1; theatre boxes as reading nooks, stage cafe, ceiling fresco, caryatids. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ateneo_Grand_Splendid)
- **Palafoxiana**: see Q1 (shelf/floor-plan data not retrieved). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Palafoxiana)

### Inferences
- Palette (my synthesis): LoC = marble polychrome + gold dome; NYPL = warm cream plaster, oak, brass/bronze lamps; Bates = green lamps, oak, sandstone vault; Beinecke = amber glow marble; Peabody = white cast iron, black-and-white floor.
- Easiest geometry: Kanazawa (box 45x45x12), Beinecke (1:2:3 box + tower), NYPL (single 297x78x52 ft nave), Bates (barrel vault 218x42x50 ft). Hardest: Tianjin terraces, Alexandria tilted disc, Peabody 5 tier ironwork, LoC statuary and mural.

- LoC interior diameter, desk count, lamp spec; Gabinete length/width/height/tiers; Morgan room dimensions; Parliament diameter/height; Qarawiyyin plan; Rampur/Khuda Bakhsh interiors; NLC reading hall dimensions; Qatar shelf/floor dims. Not found in sources fetched. HABS drawings (below) likely contain LoC and NYPL dims.
- NYPL bronze/brass lamp spec and table count not retrieved. LoC green lamps not verified by source.

## 6. Legal constraints on reproducing interiors

### Takeaway
No jurisdiction-specific legal research was completed. Treat everything as unverified; recommended default: build from PD sources (HABS, LoC) and avoid modern copyrighted architecture without permission.

### Cited Findings
- US federal works (HABS, LoC staff, Highsmith donation) are public domain/rights-free — [LoC HABS portal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/highlights.html) (portal; rights wording from my memory, not quoted).
- NYPL lions "Patience and Fortitude" are 1910s-era sculptures nicknamed by La Guardia; no trademark finding retrieved. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_A._Schwarzman_Building)

### Inferences (background knowledge, NOT sourced here)
- Pre-1930s architecture (Peabody, LoC, NYPL, BPL, Morgan, Gabinete) is out of copyright in architect-death terms in most places; modern buildings (Beinecke 1963, Seattle, QNL, Tianjin, Alexandria, Kanazawa, Vasconcelos, Parliament no) are under copyright, and many countries (e.g., France not here; China, Egypt, Qatar) have narrow or no interior freedom of panorama; US protects architectural works (AWCPA) but exempts pictorial representations of buildings visible from public places (interiors of public-access buildings not clearly covered). Replicas (3D models) are not "pictorial", so permission or fair treatment is a risk for modern works. Contested; needs legal review.
- Artworks inside (Blashfield mural, Orozco "Ballena", Mowbray) need separate check; Orozco is living-artist copyright.

### Gaps
- Per-country freedom-of-panorama rules, institutional policies, trademark status for NYPL lions, Bibliotheca Alexandrina and OMA/MVRDV licensing stances. Not researched.
