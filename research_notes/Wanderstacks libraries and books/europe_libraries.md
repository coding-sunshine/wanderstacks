# Europe's Iconic Libraries as 3D Replica Candidates (Wanderstacks)

Scope note: research was snippet-level (WebSearch/WebFetch summaries, ~35 calls). Many numeric dimensions, Commons licenses, Sketchfab/Matterport listings and per-country interior-photo law were NOT verified from primary pages. These are listed under Gaps rather than guessed. Items marked "(background, unverified)" in Inferences come from general knowledge and must be checked before use.

## 1. Location, dates, architect, style, and visual signature (per library)

### Takeaway
Core facts for most halls are confirmed, but exact interior dimensions are only confirmed for Admont, Strahov Philosophical Hall, El Escorial, Prunksaal, Wiblingen, Sistine Hall, Mafra and (length only) Long Room. Hard numbers for Klementinum, Melk, St. Gallen, Joanina, Weimar, Stockholm, Duke Humfrey's and the Radcliffe Camera need a second pass (laser-scan papers, official brochures, Structurae).

### Cited Findings

**Trinity College Dublin, Long Room (Ireland)**
- Built 1712-1733 (Wikipedia gives 1709-1732 for the whole Old Library), designed by Thomas Burgh; Long Room is nearly 65 m long and normally holds 200,000 of the oldest books — [Naomi Clifford](https://www.naomiclifford.com/the-long-room-at-trinity-college-library-dublin/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Library,_Trinity_College_Dublin)
- Original flat ceiling replaced by barrel vault plus an added upper gallery of bookcases on granite columns, by Thomas Newenham Deane and Benjamin Woodward, 1859-1861 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Library,_Trinity_College_Dublin)
- Building height 20 m, 27 bays, 3 floors; facade stone originally calp limestone and sandstone, later granite from Ballyknockan — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Library,_Trinity_College_Dublin)
- Marble busts: collection began 1743 with 14 busts commissioned from Peter Scheemakers; four female busts (Rosalind Franklin, Augusta Gregory, Ada Lovelace, Mary Wollstonecraft) added 2023 — [Naomi Clifford](https://www.naomiclifford.com/the-long-room-at-trinity-college-library-dublin/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Library,_Trinity_College_Dublin)
- Old Library holds 350,000 early printed books and 20,000 manuscript/archive collections; Book of Kells (9th c.) displayed there — [Smithsonian/University Times via search](https://universitytimes.ie/2026/03/printing-house-restored-to-house-book-of-kells/)

**Admont Abbey Library (Austria)**
- Hall 70 m long, 14 m wide, 13 m high; architect Joseph Hueber, 1776; late-Baroque/Rococo white-and-gold; ceiling frescoes by Bartolomeo Altomonte; seven cupolas; Josef Stammel sculptures — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admont_Abbey)
- About 70,000 volumes in the hall; total ~200,000 books, 1,400+ manuscripts, 900+ incunabula — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admont_Abbey)
- Restored 2005-2008 under the Federal Monuments Office; 2026 is the 250th anniversary of completion — [Stift Admont](https://stiftadmont.at/en/restoration/)

**Abbey Library of St. Gall (Switzerland)**
- Barocksaal built 1758-1767, Rococo, architect Peter Thumb; Greek inscription over door "Psyches iatreion" (healing place for the soul); carved cherubs on cases show subject (telescopes for astronomy); UNESCO WHS 1983 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Library_of_Saint_Gall)
- Visitors wear felt overshoes to protect the inlaid wooden floor — [audiala/alpen-guide summary via search](https://audiala.com/de/schweiz/st-gallen/stiftsbibliothek-st-gallen)
- Holdings: ~160,000 volumes, 1,650 incunabula, ~2,100 manuscripts (8th-15th c.), Nibelungenlied Manuscript B, Egyptian mummy Shep-en-Isis; 900+ manuscripts online via e-codices — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Library_of_Saint_Gall)

**Strahov Library, Prague (Czech Republic)**
- Theological Hall completed 1679 under Abbot Jeroným Hirnhaim; ceiling paintings (1720s) by Siard Nosecky on biblical wisdom themes; Philosophical Hall completed 1779 under Abbot Vaclav Mayer, Classical — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strahov_Monastery); [Strahov official](https://www.strahovskyklaster.cz/en/architecture/library-halls)
- Philosophical Hall 32 m long, 10 m wide, 14 m high; walnut shelving bought from dissolved Louka monastery near Znojmo, hall built to fit the shelves; Maulbertsch ceiling fresco painted in six months of 1794 with one assistant — [Strahov official](https://www.strahovskyklaster.cz/en/philosophical-hall)
- 200,000+ volumes, 3,000+ manuscripts, 1,500 first prints in depository — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strahov_Monastery)
- Library reconstruction 2009-2010 — [Strahov](https://strahovskyklaster.cz/en/restoration-of-monuments/reconstruction-of-the-strahov-library-20092010)

**Bodleian: Duke Humfrey's, Radcliffe Camera, Divinity School (UK)**
- Duke Humfrey's: built as second storey over Divinity School 1450-1480; Arts End 1610-1612; Selden End ~20 years later; H-shaped plan; Humfrey gave 281 books (university then had ~20); used as Hogwarts library — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Humfrey%27s_Library)
- Radcliffe Camera: James Gibbs, Baroque, begun 17 May 1737, opened 13 April 1749; Headington and Burford stone, lead dome (41 tonnes Derbyshire lead); Rysbrack statue of Radcliffe; tunnel to underground stacks; not open to general public — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Camera)

**Royal Library of El Escorial (Spain)**
- Main hall (Salon de los Impresos / Salon de los Frescos) about 54-59 m long, 9 m wide, 10 m high (sources conflict); Herrera designed exotic-wood shelving; Tibaldi painted barrel vault with allegories of the seven liberal arts, program attributed to Arias Montano and Herrera — [search summary of Smarthistory/Columbia/others](https://smarthistory.org/el-escorial); [Columbia](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/spanish/courses/spanish3349/06augeimperio/arte_escorial.html)

**Biblioteca Joanina, Coimbra (Portugal)**
- Commissioned by John V, built 1717-1728; three-storey building; Brazilian/exotic woods, painted ceilings by Simoes Ribeiro and Vicente Nunes; portrait of John V by Domenico Dupra; walls 2.11 m thick; teak door; 18-20 C and ~60% humidity; bats control insects (also Mafra) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Joanina) (architect named there as Joao Carvalho Ferreira; other sources say Gaspar Ferreira, conflict not resolved)
- 70,000 to 250,000 volumes (source gives a wide range) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Joanina)

**Prunksaal, Austrian National Library (Austria)**
- Begun 1721 by Johann Bernhard Fischer von Erlach, finished 1723-26 by son Joseph Emanuel; sculpture Lorenzo Mattielli; frescoes by Daniel Gran 1726-1730; four Coronelli globes; marble emperor statues with Charles VI central; Pacassi iron ring after dome cracks, Maulbertsch restored frescoes — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prunksaal); [ONB PDF](https://webarchiv.onb.ac.at/web/20150803104515/http://www.onb.ac.at/files/Prunksaal_en.pdf)
- Over 77 m long; oval dome floor plan 18 x 29.2 m, 29.2 m high; 200,000+ books — [search summary of ONB/visitingvienna](https://www.visitingvienna.com/sights/winter-palace/prunksaal/)

**Melk Abbey Library (Austria)**
- Within Jakob Prandtauer's Baroque abbey (1702-1736); two main rooms with ceiling frescoes by Paul Troger 1731-1732; restoration 1978-1995 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melk_Abbey); [search summary](https://austria-forum.org/af/AustriaWiki/Paul_Troger)
- 2019 find: fragment of "Der Rosendorn" c.1300 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melk_Abbey)

**Klementinum Baroque Library Hall, Prague**
- Completed 1722 in Jesuit building phase; single long two-storey hall, ceiling fresco by Jan Hiebl ("Temple of Wisdom"), walnut shelving, terrestrial and celestial globes, ~20,000 volumes — [Prague City Tourism](https://www.praguecitytourism.cz/en/our-services/the-towers-of-prague/visit-our-towers/the-clementinum-astronomical-tower-and-baroque-library-19514); [Culture Trip](https://theculturetrip.com/articles/5-photos-that-prove-prague-has-the-worlds-most-beautiful-library)

**Mafra Palace Library (Portugal)**
- Main room 83 m long, longest in complex; Rococo stucco and cream-coloured wood; 36,000 leather volumes incl. incunabula; two-storey shelves designed by Manuel Caetano de Sousa; bat colony — [Ericeira Mag / FSU exhibit via search](https://www.ericeiramag.pt/en/?p=1779); [FSU](https://lis5472.cci.fsu.edu/sp21/group6/exhibits/show/libraries-in-europe/mafra-palace-library)

**Wiblingen Abbey Library (Germany)**
- Completed 1744 per one summary (the hall is usually dated to 1740s-1750s Rococo/early Neoclassical; unresolved); 72 m by 27 m; ceiling frescoes by Januarius Zick; architect Franz Martin Kuen; gallery with slender columns, extensive stucco, natural lighting — [search summary of state palaces brochure](https://www.kloster-wiblingen.de/en/monastery); [Schloesser und Gaerten brochure](https://www.schloesser-und-gaerten.de/fileadmin/Broschueren/monumentflyer_2023-24_englisch/ssg_kloster-wiblingen_monumentbroschuere-GB_2023-24.pdf)

**John Rylands Library, Manchester (UK)**
- Late-Victorian neo-Gothic by Basil Champneys, hand-carved Cumbrian sandstone, ten-year build, opened 1 Jan 1900, cost about 200,000 pounds — [Wikipedia mirror](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Rylands_Library); [Univ. of Manchester](https://www.library.manchester.ac.uk/rylands/about/our-history)

**Vatican Apostolic Library, Sistine Hall**
- Commissioned by Sixtus V c.1587, architect Domenico Fontana; hall about 70 x 15 m (another source 80 x 16 m); fresco program with views of Rome in pendentives and lunettes; single hall once the largest library — [History of Information](https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=2727); [Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2017/10/27/this-single-room-in-the-vatican-library-was-once-the-largest-library-in-the-world-it-now-reopens-as-a-reading-room/)

**BnF Richelieu, Paris**
- Salle Labrouste (Henri Labrouste, 1868): nine ceramic-tile domes with oculi on openwork iron arches, 16 slender cast-iron columns about 10 m high — [BnF](https://www.bnf.fr/en/richelieu-site-palace-heart-paris); [Archiseek](https://www.archiseek.com/?p=1074151)
- Salle Ovale: Jean-Louis Pascal began 1897, completed 1932 by Alfred Recoura, inaugurated December 1936; 16 pairs of fluted Ionic cast-iron columns — [BnF](https://www.bnf.fr/en/richelieu-site-palace-heart-paris)
- Site renovated by Bruno Gaudin (reopened to public 2022, background, see Gaps) — [Metropolis](https://metropolismag.com/projects/paris-richelieu-complex-re-opens-after-stunning-renovation/)

**Stockholm Public Library (Sweden)**
- Gunnar Asplund; designed 1922, built from 1924, opened 31 March 1928; west wing 1932; classicism-to-functionalism transition; cylindrical rotunda; Sweden's first open-shelf public library; ~1.5 million books — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_City_Library)
- A laser-scan/archival-plans paper on this building exists — [Cambridge Core PDF](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/002EFCA0E116C7264C4EF13280E0D2AE/S1359135523000179a.pdf/archival-plans-alterations-and-3d-laser-scanning-of-erik-gunnar-asplunds-stockholm-public-library.pdf)

**Duchess Anna Amalia Library, Rococo Hall, Weimar (Germany)**
- Green Castle 1562-1565 (Nikolaus Gromann); converted to library 1766 per plans of August Friedrich Strassburger; oval Rococo hall with portrait of Carl August; UNESCO 1998 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchess_Anna_Amalia_Library); [search summary](https://www.weimar.de/en/culture/sights/museums/herzogin-anna-amalia-bibliothek/)
- Fire 2 Sept 2004 destroyed ~50,000 volumes; reopened 24 Oct 2007 after about USD 18.2m restoration — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchess_Anna_Amalia_Library)

**Royal Danish Library, Black Diamond (Denmark)**
- Schmidt Hammer Lassen, built 1995-1999, opened 15 Sept 1999; 20,733 m2, 7 floors; 2,500 m2 Absolute Black granite from Zimbabwe; top-lit atrium with wavy balconies; Per Kirkeby 210 m2 oil ceiling fresco at reading room entrance; six reading rooms — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Diamond_(library))

### Inferences
- A builder can start fully parametric models for Admont (70x14x13), Prunksaal (77+ long, oval dome 18x29.2), Wiblingen (72x27), Mafra (83 long), Strahov Philosophical (32x10x14), Escorial (~54-59x9x10).
- Long Room: ~65 m length plus 20 m building height suggests a ~12 m clear nave height under the vault (background, unverified; measure from photos or scans).
- Background, unverified: St. Gallen hall ~ 30 x 10 m, Strahov Theological ~ 32 x 10 m, Klementinum ~ 20 x 10 m. Do not use without checking.

- Wiblingen completion year conflict; Joanina architect-name conflict; Escorial length conflict (54 vs 59 m).

## 5. Legal constraints on reproducing interiors

### Takeaway
Interior replicas of old halls are low-risk for architecture copyright (all pre-1900s, architects long dead; copyright lasts life+70). Risk sits in photo copyright (photographers' images), institutional image policies, and modern buildings. Black Diamond (1999) is certainly still protected. Stockholm: Asplund died 1940, so life+70 likely expired end of 2010 (background, unverified; Stockholm's later alterations may carry separate rights). BnF Richelieu's 2022 Gaudin renovation is likely protected (unverified).

### Cited Findings
- Commons FoP summary: Austria allows reproducing architecture including interiors; Germany limits to exteriors; Netherlands covers public building interiors — [Commons FoP Europe](https://c.enwp.org/wiki/Commons:Freedom_of_panorama/Europe)
- St. Gallen requires permission for professional photo/film — [Stiftsbezirk](https://www.stiftsbezirk.ch/userdata/Stiftsbibliothek/Benutzung/Foto- und Filmaufnahmen/reglement-foto-und-film-de.pdf)
- Joanina prohibits photography inside — [visit.uc.pt](https://visit.uc.pt/en/info)

### Inferences
- A from-scratch model built from measurements and public-domain/CC images avoids FoP issues, since modeling is a derivative of the (expired) architectural work, not of photos. Counsel check advised.
- Black Diamond (1999, SHL) and Kirkeby fresco are in copyright; Danish FoP status for interiors unverified. Skip or seek permission.
- Trademarks: "Book of Kells", "Long Room" and institutional logos may have usage policies (background, unverified).

### Gaps
- No country-by-country interior law verified for UK, Ireland, Czechia, Portugal, Spain, Italy/Vatican, Sweden, France, Denmark. Check Commons FoP page per country.
