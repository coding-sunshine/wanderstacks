# Fly in, walk round, step inside

**Build the visit as one three.js scene and one canvas: a van Wijk flight from the globe lands at a cheap exterior, the visitor walks the forecourt under the real sun and weather, then dissolves through the door into the existing parametric hall.** Gaussian-splat captures are an optional upgrade layer, because the notes found no library whose capture you can legally self-host. Sun position costs nothing (SunCalc, a few lines per frame), and weather costs one scheduled Worker call per refresh using Open-Meteo, which is free for non-profit sites with attribution. The binding constraints are licences and file size, not rendering. Cloudflare Pages caps every asset at 25 MiB, so a room splat must be an SOG or SPZ file of roughly 1M splats or less, or live on R2. Navigation should be tap-to-glide plus drag-to-look, with only curated books pullable, a parallel 2D index, and a DOM reader for the text. The figmaformotion.com templates are not worth buying for this build. Much of the evidence is thin: several notes are marked partial, performance numbers for mid-range phones are missing, and design guidance is flagged below as inference where no source backed it.

## Sun and weather cost almost nothing, if a Worker owns the cache

For position, **SunCalc** (BSD-2) returns sun and moon position, phase and rise/set times, with stated error of about 0.08 degrees for the sun ([mourner/suncalc](https://github.com/mourner/suncalc)). astronomy-engine (MIT) is more accurate but is a **116,485-byte** minified bundle ([cosinekitty/astronomy](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy)). The sun's disc is about 0.5 degrees wide, so no shadow can show the difference; pick SunCalc for size. One caveat: the README mentions a 2.0 rewrite whose npm availability was not confirmed.

For weather, **Open-Meteo** fits best. Its free tier covers "private or non-profit websites or apps that do not have subscriptions or advertising", with limits of 600 calls a minute and **10,000 a day**, under CC BY 4.0 ([Open-Meteo terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms)). The `current` block exposes cloud cover, precipitation, snowfall, weather code and day/night flag, built from 15-minute model data ([docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)). Browser CORS support is only indirectly evidenced by a merged fix ([GitHub](https://github.com/open-meteo/open-meteo/issues?q=CORS)). MET Norway is the backup (CC BY 4.0, but it requires an identifying User-Agent and coordinates capped at four decimals, per its [terms](https://api.met.no/doc/TermsOfService)). The US NWS API rejects browsers that send a custom User-Agent, so it needs a proxy anyway ([discussion](https://github.com/weather-gov/api/discussions/312)). Keyed providers such as Pirate Weather (10,000 calls a month) and OpenWeather add nothing here except a secret to hide.

The arithmetic argues for a scheduled batch rather than per-visitor calls. A hundred libraries refreshed four times an hour is about 9,600 upstream calls a day, nearly the whole Open-Meteo allowance. Workers Free allows 100,000 requests a day, with 10 ms CPU and 50 subrequests per request ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). My inference is that a Cron-triggered Worker should fetch all libraries in one multi-location call every 30 minutes (about 48 calls a day) and write one JSON blob to KV, which a secondary source puts at 1,000 free writes a day ([eastondev](https://eastondev.com/blog/en/posts/dev/20260526-cloudflare-free-limits/)). The Cache API is per datacentre, so it multiplies upstream calls with global traffic. Multi-location support and cron free-tier limits were not verified. The client reads `/api/weather` from its own origin and, if that fails, falls back to clear sky computed from SunCalc, so the site never depends on a third party. Weather for a lost library such as Alexandria is simply weather at its coordinates; no precedent for historical skies was found, so the page should say "now".

On screen, three's Preetham `Sky` (WebGL) takes a sun position and turbidity ([Sky docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Sky.html)), and the sun becomes a shadow-casting directional light through the windows. The notes sourced none of the following, so treat it as proposal: capture the sky into a PMREM environment map only when the sun moves noticeably, scale sun intensity and turbidity by cloud cover, fake god rays with additive window cones, draw rain and snow as near-camera instanced points, and fade lamps in as the sun drops below about -4 degrees. On phones skip volumetric clouds and the takram atmosphere packages, which are beta ([three-geospatial](https://github.com/takram-design-engineering/three-geospatial)).

## No library has a self-hostable capture, so licences decide the layer

The per-library search was cut short, and the honest result is that **none of the 36 libraries has a confirmed capture you may host**. Trinity's Long Room has an official 360 tour ([TCD](https://www.tcd.ie/library/using-library/360.php)) and a commercial one ([Panoramic Ireland](https://panoramicireland.com/guide-to-ireland/360-degree-panoramas-of-ireland/trinity-college-360-degree-panorama)); both are link-only. A Sketchfab search for "Long Room Trinity" returned zero models ([API query](https://api.sketchfab.com/v3/search?type=models&q=Long%20Room%20Trinity&count=10)). The earlier libraries report found only three downloadable library interiors, all non-commercial (Rylands, State Library Victoria, a generic NYPL). Most of the 29 remaining libraries were never searched.

Platform terms rule out re-hosting other people's captures. Luma's free tier grants Luma broad rights over your input and bars distributing output through non-Luma marketplaces without commercial permission ([Luma ToS](https://lumalabs.ai/legal/tos)). Scaniverse gives Niantic a perpetual, sublicensable licence and states no public-sharing licence ([Niantic Spatial](https://nianticspatial.com/terms/)). CyArk data is CC BY-NC ([CyArk](https://www.cyark.org/data-use-policy)), Open Heritage 3D says most datasets prohibit commercial use ([FAQ](https://openheritage3d.org/faq)), and Zamani is "All Rights Reserved" ([site](https://zamaniproject.org/)). Google's 3D Tiles allow no offline use or caching beyond headers and must show attribution ([policy](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies)); the free allowance is **1,000 events a month** ([pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)), so it is a click-to-load extra for a few standing buildings, not the exterior. Google's Maps Embed is free and unlimited but is an iframe with an API key; it cannot be fused into your scene ([Embed billing](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/embed/usage-and-billing)).

That leaves three routes. First, your own capture where a library allows photography: Brush runs on macOS and WebGPU, and OpenSplat runs on Metal but is AGPLv3 ([Brush](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArthurBrussee/brush/main/README.md), [OpenSplat](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pierotofy/OpenSplat/main/README.md)). A reasonable pipeline, which I inferred and did not test, is COLMAP poses, then Brush, then SOG or SPZ. Second, Commons photos, but this is unproven: the in-the-wild research that works (WildGaussians) used landmarks with many photos and still struggles where regions are hidden in nearly all images ([arXiv 2407.08447](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08447v2)), and I found nothing on 20 to 100 interior photos. CC BY-SA defines adapted material broadly ([legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)), so a splat from BY-SA photos plausibly inherits BY-SA, and NC or ND photos must be excluded; no source addresses splats directly, and this is not legal advice. Third, the cheapest and safest route: a hand-built exterior proxy of an old building and the parametric hall inside. Freedom of panorama covers buildings in Ireland, Austria, the US, UK, India and Czechia but not France or Italy, and no source says whether it covers an interactive 3D reconstruction ([Commons FOP](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Freedom_of_panorama)). Lead with old exteriors and treat Austrian interiors as the riskiest.

For delivery, Spark (MIT) reads PLY, SPZ, SPLAT, KSPLAT and SOG, defaults to a **500K to 2.5M** splat budget by device, and streams LoD trees from `.RAD` files with HTTP Range requests ([Spark 2.0](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/new-features-2.0/)). SOG is 15 to 20 times smaller than PLY ([PlayCanvas](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/gaussian-splatting/formats/)). Pages allows **25 MiB per file** and 20,000 files ([limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)); R2 adds 10 GB, 10M reads a month and free egress ([pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). My arithmetic from those ratios puts a 1M-splat room at about 12 to 25 MB, so it fits one Pages file with little spare. Spark's compatibility with the repo's three.js version is untested.

## Walk the forecourt first, then step through the door

Exterior-first suits the constraints. Globe and exterior share a renderer, so there is no context swap, and the exterior asset stays small and off the interior's critical path. My recommended layering is a low-poly exterior proxy with a walkable forecourt (roaming is a simple capsule on a flat plane) lit by the same sun, with the room splat or parametric hall loading in the background. Stylised OSM-derived extrusions for the surrounding street are an option; the notes did not research them. Reversed-Z depth must be chosen when the renderer is created, and it cannot coexist with the logarithmic depth buffer ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/can-three-js-dynamically-set-reverseddepthbuffer-what-is-the-proper-way-to-do-it/90217)), which is another reason for one renderer shared by globe and building.

The flight itself has good precedent. MapLibre's `flyTo` implements van Wijk and Nuij's zoom-pan curve and falls back to `easeTo` for tiny distances ([camera.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/main/src/ui/camera.ts)). Cesium picks a duration from distance, bends pitch downward at the apex so Earth stays in frame, and eases cubic-out when high and quintic-in-out when low ([CameraFlightPath.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CesiumGS/cesium/main/packages/engine/Source/Scene/CameraFlightPath.js), [Camera docs](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Camera.html)). For a custom globe, interpolate altitude in log space so apparent speed stays constant, and cap the duration at a few seconds (my design choice, unsourced). The flight doubles as a loading screen: Spark's worker-side LoD traversal means the coarse root chunk can arrive during flight and refine after landing ([Spark LoD](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/new-spark-renderer/)). Start the fetch on pin hover or click.

For the final step through the door, Aircord's Awwwards case study layered five render targets, each with its own camera, composited by one timeline ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/aircord-case-study.html)). Because Spark splats render in the same three.js scene as meshes, I expect a doorway-shaped alpha dissolve in the last half second, with the interior camera pre-placed at the same pose, to hide the handoff; depth behaviour between splats and meshes is unverified. The View Transitions API belongs on DOM overlays only, never the canvas ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions)). Most stutter comes from first-use work: `compileAsync` compiles shaders but not textures, which upload on first render, and a 4096 by 4096 PNG occupies about **88 MB** of VRAM unless compressed to KTX2 ([forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/reduce-first-render-lag-renderer-compileasync-is-not-doing-anything/86191)). Warm every scene, render target and texture during the globe phase. As a fail-safe, if average frame time exceeds about 24 ms during flight, cut to the arrival pose with a crossfade (unsourced thresholds). Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, replace travel with a 300 to 500 ms crossfade, as WCAG 2.3.3 expects interaction motion to be disableable ([W3C](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/animation-from-interactions)).

The figmaformotion.com v2 site sells editable Figma animation templates by Daniel Snows, Basic at $9.90 and Premium at $59.90 (the homepage showed $44.95 in a sale), plus 11 AI prompts that turn the designs into sites ([v2](https://figmaformotion.com/v2), [home](https://figmaformotion.com)). It contains no three.js or camera technique, its licence forbids redistributing template files, and the prompt text is not public. Skip it. What its genre offers, staged DOM reveals, shared-element morphs from pin to card, and text staggered to camera phases, is reproducible with CSS, the Web Animations API and View Transitions. That list is inferred from template names, since no file was opened.

## Movement, book picking and reading: keep it simple and optional

Accessibility guidance is the strongest source. Head bob, shake, motion blur and automatic camera moves cause motion sickness through visual-vestibular mismatch, and FOV, bob and sensitivity should be adjustable ([Xbox XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)). Large repeated textures are another trigger, which is an art fix for tiling floors ([Madeline Miller](https://madelinemiller.dev/blog/motion-sickness-accessibility/)). Museum-tour research finds point-to-point hotspot navigation frustrating because users hunt for arrows (search summary of [arXiv 2310.11176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11176)), while Shapespark's shipped walkthroughs combine click-to-walk, WASD and tap-to-teleport on touch ([Shapespark](https://www.shapespark.com/blog/sell-properties-faster-with-browser-based-virtual-walkthroughs)). My synthesis, flagged as opinion: let any floor point be a valid target, glide there with eased start and stop over 0.6 to 1.2 s, support drag-look, WASD and an optional joystick, ship bob off, and keep walking near 1.4 m/s. Exact eye height, FOV and bob values were not found in any source.

Gone Home is the best-documented model for picking books. Loose books are interactable and books wedged in shelves are not, which tells players what matters and avoids hundreds of readable texts. A Put Back action returns objects, and interactables show a text prompt ([Fullbright](https://fullbright.company/2012/09/28/a-non-violent-immersive-sim)). NN/g found users spent 22% more time on flat designs working out what was clickable ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/clickable-elements/)), though the figure came from a search summary. So make only curated books pullable and mark them with a protruding spine, brighter edge or a lamp glow, with a hover label. The pull sequence (spine slides out, book lifts to the camera, room dims, Esc returns it to its exact slot) is my proposal, and the open animation should be short, since Skyrim players modded theirs faster ([mod](https://stepmodifications.org/wiki/SkyrimSE:Convenient%20Reading%20UI%20-%20SE)).

For listing, a 3D shelf alone fails when a visitor wants to find a title. Harvard's Stack View renders spines from catalogue metadata, with page count as thickness, to recreate scanning and structured serendipity ([Code4Lib](https://code4lib.org/conference/2012/cain/)). That evidence covers large catalogues, not 5 to 50 curated books. I recommend varied real spine sizes, plus a keyboard-reachable 2D index (title, author, year, length) whose click flies the camera to that shelf. It doubles as search and as the accessibility path, and a "Browse as list" entry under reduced motion should land on the same reader. A short optional guided tour of three to five stops has a shipped precedent in Assassin's Creed Origins' Discovery Tour, which offers 75 tours of 5 to 20 minutes ([Ubisoft](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/13sXevEgX278m7jxcX5uhM/assassins-creed-origins-discovery-tour-shows-a-different-side-of-ancient-egypt)).

For reading, one Waikato study found a book-simulating interface preferred and faster with no loss of accuracy, though only the abstract was read ([Waikato](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/handle/10289/4739)). Xbox guidance warns against moving content behind text and asks for an opaque background option ([XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)). The design that follows: the 3D book opens to its cover and flies to the camera, then crossfades to a DOM reader with real selectable text, font-size control, 55 to 75 character lines, keyboard control and a visible Back to shelf. The page-turn flourish stays on the 3D book only, under about 400 ms (all unsourced specifics).

## Conclusion

The research reframes "photoreal" for a free static site. Photorealism cannot come from third-party capture, because every route met a licence wall, a metering wall or a missing file, so the free path is to make light real, not geometry. Real sun, real cloud cover and a believable sky through the windows of a parametric hall cost two small libraries and one cron job, and they will read as "live" more than a mediocre splat would. Splats are best treated as a per-library upgrade, shipped only where a rights-clean capture exists or you take one yourself.

The open risks are measurement, not concept: no mid-range phone frame times for Spark, no real SOG sizes for a captured room, untested Spark and mesh depth blending at the doorway, and unverified Open-Meteo CORS and multi-location behaviour. Prove those four first, in that order, on one library, before adding a second.
