# Build WorldLibs rooms that load like games

**Make the globe a hand-tuned, sub-4 MB NASA-texture planet; make each library a seeded parametric hall that your visitor's device builds on arrival; and use AI at build time only, to propose evidence-checked parameters and a few props, never to invent what the visitor reads.** That is the whole plan in one line. The research supports it for four reasons. (1) Roblox's "dynamic worlds" are really streamed, server-baked worlds, so the copyable lessons are shell-first loading and gating at the loaded edge, not client-side generation. (2) Procedural libraries are proven to be tiny: the Total Library game ships an infinite library in about 17-18 MB including its engine. (3) The only AI world tool with a clean static-hosting path is World Labs Marble, and its splats must be size-tested against your 25 MB room cap. (4) Google's photoreal 3D Tiles are too metered (1,000 free sessions a month) to be the entry globe. Phase A (globe + Long Room) should also ship one Living Page (Byrne's Euclid) and the reusable predict/reveal/stamp component, because that component is what makes the other Living Pages cheap. Seven items in the notes are unresolved and could change this plan (see the verification table at the end). Convention used throughout: a citation means a note sourced it; **(I)** marks my inference or design proposal, which no note verified.

## The globe: spend the 5 MB on light, not on detail

Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles work from three.js (via NASA-AMMOS 3DTilesRendererJS, Apache-2.0) but sit in the Enterprise SKU, with **1,000 free root-tile requests a month, then $6 per 1,000**; offline use, bulk extraction and caching beyond response headers are prohibited, and the Google logo and attributions must stay visible ([Google pricing](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing), [Map Tiles policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies), [3DTilesRendererJS](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS)). A public site would burn 1,000 sessions quickly, and several of your sites (Alexandria, Nalanda) are ruins that a modern city mesh cannot show. The right use is a click-to-load "see it today" toggle on a few real, standing places, behind a referrer-restricted key and a hard daily quota **(I)**. It is not the base globe.

The base globe should be custom three.js with self-hosted public-domain NASA imagery. NASA content is generally uncopyrighted in the US and only needs acknowledgement ([NASA media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)); Blue Marble Next Generation gives twelve months of true-colour imagery ([NASA Earth Observatory](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/collections/blue-marble)). The realism stack I recommend is cheap and well-understood: day/night mix by `dot(normal, sunDir)` with a smoothstep terminator, Black Marble city lights multiplied by (1 - day), ocean specular from a land mask, one slowly drifting 2k cloud layer, a small relief map, and a Fresnel atmosphere rim **(I, standard technique, not sourced in the notes)**. Budget estimate: 4k day + 4k night + 2k mask/cloud/relief as KTX2 lands near **3-4 MB** **(I, unmeasured)**. The physically based option, `@takram/three-atmosphere` (MIT), is Beta and tied to a WebGPU rewrite, so its WebGL2 path and mobile cost need confirming before you depend on it ([three-geospatial](https://github.com/takram-design-engineering/three-geospatial)).

Two moves make the globe feel alive rather than merely pretty. First, drive the terminator from the same time slider that controls the library timeline, so scrubbing 1700 to 2026 also moves the sun **(I)**. Second, treat the fly-in as a loading screen: slerp along the great circle with a raise-then-drop height arc and start fetching the room during the flight **(I; MapLibre's `flyTo` already implements the van Wijk-Nuij arc if you ever adopt it)**. For deep zoom, stream OpenFreeMap vector tiles (free, no request limits, attribution required) or EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, fine for a non-commercial site, but do not bake it into your bundle) only after the camera drops below globe level ([OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/), [EOX licence](https://cloudless.eox.at/documentation/license)). MapLibre v5 now has a globe projection with atmosphere, which is the fastest high-zoom route but a second renderer ([MapLibre v5](https://newreleases.io/project/github/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/release/v5.0.0)).

For time, store `{founded, destroyed|null}` per library and ghost pins that are destroyed or not yet founded **(I)**. Pleiades (CC BY) supplies ancient coordinates ([NYU archive](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/74877)). Historical borders are the one real legal fork: `aourednik/historical-basemaps` is GPL-3.0, with disputed borders and a precision field ([GitHub](https://github.com/aourednik/historical-basemaps)); OpenHistoricalMap data is mostly CC0 ([OHM](https://openhistoricalmap.org/copyright)). Skip borders in Phase A, and prefer OHM if you add them.

## The rooms: parametric shells, baked light, real documents

The Roblox lesson is structural. Streaming loads content by player position, keeps a never-evicted inner radius, and offers `PauseOutsideLoadedArea` so players cannot walk into unloaded space ([Roblox streaming docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/workspace/streaming.md)). Its far-LOD (SLIM) is baked in the cloud at publish time and cut one example from about 2.6M to 170k triangles ([SLIM docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/workspace/streaming/slim)). Nothing is generated on the client. The WorldLibs equivalent is a **build-time shell and client-time detail split**: the hall shell, shelves and books come from a few KB of kit parameters generated in a Worker with a seeded PRNG; lightmaps, shared texture atlases, splats and unique props are baked at build time; the book instances are never downloaded **(I, assembled from the notes' table)**. Use one `InstancedMesh` per bay (so built-in culling works and generation can be chunked), 2-4 book geometries, per-instance colour, and spine titles from one shared atlas ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-to-choose-between-instancedmesh-and-batchedmesh/81221)). Hold the doorway until the next bay is built, the Roblox pause rule applied to a hall **(I)**.

On realism, the notes' best-supported pipeline is: parametric hall, then offline path-traced lightmaps with `three-gpu-pathtracer`, three-mesh-bvh and xatlas, shipped as KTX2, in two variants (daylight and candlelight) ([three-lightmap-baker](https://github.com/gonnavis/three-lightmap-baker), [bake guide](https://www.svilenkovic.com/3d/how-to-bake-lighting-for-web)). The evidence here is thin (one demo, one tutorial), so prove it on the Long Room first. Gaussian splats are the "photoreal" option but come with limits. Spark 2.0 (MIT, three.js) has LoD, streaming `.RAD` files with a coarse first chunk, and budgets of about 500k splats on mobile ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/world-labs-releases-spark-v2.0)); SPZ is about 10x smaller than PLY ([Niantic SPZ 4](https://www.nianticspatial.com/blog/spz4)). But splats are baked radiance (no relighting, weak on gilding and glass), Commons photo sets rarely give clean interior coverage, and a splat from CC BY-SA photos is plausibly a derivative **(I)**. Use splats for a hero zone or backdrop only. No openly licensed scan of the Long Room, Admont or Strahov turned up; the cleanest model found is the CC BY Library of Celsus (with a "NoAI" flag, so keep it out of any generative pipeline) ([Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/library-of-celsus-crowdsourced-photogrammetry-1cc6ad6af508422398f60f902cb70e88)).

Cultural significance is where a walkable room can beat a photo, and the notes point to documents and testimony rather than set dressing. The Digital Library Cave puts 21 zoomable documents and real historical figures inside a 3D hall and logged 14M visits in a week ([China Daily](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202304/28/WS644b28b1498ea274927b93ba/digital-caves-bring-history-alive.html)); Discovery Tour offers short narrated tours with combat removed ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2018-02-13-assassins-creed-origins-discovery-tour.html)). For WorldLibs: every case holds a real readable item with a source and licence line; each room has two or three five-minute tours plus free roam; people appear as silhouettes with a footnote per figure (Philodemus, Xuanzang, the Mamma Haidara family) **(I)**. The Long Room has a ready-made story: the flat-ceilinged hall of 1712-33 got its barrel vault in 1859-61, and the Old Library closes in 2027 for restoration to about 2030, so a phase slider and a "while it is closed" framing add real value (facts from the earlier report; University Times link: [Printing House](https://universitytimes.ie/2026/03/printing-house-restored-to-house-book-of-kells/)). For sound, OpenAIR offers Creative Commons impulse responses of heritage spaces, but no library hall was found, so label any convolution reverb "proxy acoustic, not this room" ([OpenAIR](https://www.openair.hosted.york.ac.uk)).

For lost libraries, the discipline is the London Charter and Seville Principles: record paradata, declare authenticity levels, and separate real from interpreted ([London Charter](https://londoncharter.org/introduction.html), [Seville Principles](https://vwhl.luddy.indiana.edu/sevillePrinciples/index.html)). The 2026 "hallucinated heritage" literature names plausibility-over-authenticity as the central danger ([EGU26-21526](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-21526.html), via search summary). So the tag must be a **render-time attribute**: invented elements ghosted or dashed, a "show evidence only" toggle, AI-assisted assets badged with model and date, and no AI-made text, faces or inscriptions **(I)**.

## Where AI fits: build time, proposal only, never the visitor's runtime

World models first. Marble exports Gaussian splats (SPZ/PLY) plus collider and GLB meshes, and World Labs recommends Spark to render them; the Free plan cannot export, and commercial rights start at Pro ([World Labs export docs](https://docs.worldlabs.ai/marble/export/gaussian-splat)). API cost is about **$1.20 per world** (1,500 credits at $1 = 1,250 credits) ([API pricing](https://docs.worldlabs.ai/api/pricing.md)). Genie 3 is a streamed, US-only, Ultra-subscriber prototype with no export path found ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/project-genie/)). HunyuanWorld and Hunyuan3D exclude the EU, UK and South Korea in their licence territory ([Hunyuan3D-2 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2/blob/main/LICENSE)). So: Marble for lost-library mood backdrops tagged "invented", nothing else in that class. For props, TRELLIS.2 is MIT with GLB/PBR output but needs a 24 GB NVIDIA GPU, so rent one for hours ([model card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/trellis.2-4b)); Meshy, Tripo and Rodin are cheap APIs whose output licences conflict across sources and must be checked at the vendor **(I: a few dollars to under $50 for a few dozen props)**. Generate props from a licensed reference image so the source is recorded as paradata, decimate hard, and never from an inscribed object **(I)**.

The most valuable AI job is not geometry. It is the **quote-to-parameters pipeline**: a source quote with a citation row, an LLM proposing hall-kit JSON (dimensions, bays, columns), JSON-schema and numeric validation (the quote must contain the number), human sign-off, and a paradata record; elements with no quote id are auto-tagged "invented". It runs at build time and the JSON is committed **(I; no published example of this workflow was found)**. Splats from few photos (AnySplat, WildGaussians) are research-feasible but unproven on sparse Commons interiors ([AnySplat](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23716v2), [WildGaussians](https://the-decoder.com/wild-gaussians-new-ai-method-enables-3d-reconstruction-from-user-captured-web-photos/)).

Runtime AI collides with the budget. In-browser LLMs and depth models are 400 MB to 1.4 GB (a Depth Anything v2 WebGPU build is about 1.4 GB) and WebGPU-only ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/phiph/DA-2-WebGPU/blob/main/HF_MODEL_CARD.md), [tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/17/browser-native-llm-inference-webgpu)). That is 80-280x the 5 MB initial budget. Pre-written cited text has zero hallucination risk and costs nothing; keep any local narrator as a later opt-in button with retrieval over the evidence rows **(I)**.

## Living pages and game feel: one component, three pilots, no servers

Every Joule's loop is "predict, test and prove each idea, lift the flap, earn a stamp", with diagrams that lift off the page as 3D ink ([Every Joule](https://iamtechartist.com/every-joule/)). Nicky Case's "Place Your Bets" and sandbox patterns, and Distill's finding that prediction prompts and low-stakes quizzes aid recall, back the loop ([ncase.me](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/), [Distill 2020](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)). The same Distill paper warns these pages are labour-intensive, so share one predict/reveal/stamp component and keep each page to one scene **(I: about 2-4 days per page; the component itself is small)**. Proposed pilots, all my design **(I)**: Byrne's Euclid in the Long Room (guess which coloured-solid step comes next, then watch the proof unfold, then a slider sandbox); the Plan of St Gall in the St Gallen room (predict where the scriptorium or infirmary sits before the plan lifts into 3D; the manuscript Cod. Sang. 1092 is CC BY-NC 4.0 on e-codices, per the earlier research); Darwin's divergence diagram (predict which lineage splits first). Reward understanding, not completion: gamification raises engagement more than learning ([Souropetsis et al. 2023](https://clok.uclan.ac.uk/49519/1/Souropetsisetal.2023.pdf)), so keep stamps cosmetic with no streaks or leaderboards.

For game feel, the notes' reference set is small but consistent. The Uncensored Library (about 300 books, 12 wings, 25M+ visitors reported) shows one conceit, real texts and minimal UI beat feature breadth ([Minecraft.net](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/uncensored-library), [Substack summary](https://justindownes.substack.com/p/minecrafts-uncensored-virtual-library)). Gone Home shows that objects, not markers, carry story ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-gone-home-i-s-design-constraints-lead-to-a-powerful-story)). Obra Dinn's deduction ledger maps neatly onto sorting claims as attested, inferred or legend ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn)). Treat destruction as testimony, never a timer or a win state ([Play the Past](https://www.playthepast.org/?p=5618)); run a human sensitivity pass on Alexandria, Timbuktu 2013 and Weimar 2004. Accessibility modes (guided tour, free roam, reader list with no 3D; `prefers-reduced-motion` swaps fly-ins for cross-fades) are my proposal and cost little **(I)**.

Snappiness is mostly discipline about the first second. Canvas is not an LCP candidate, so make a pre-rendered globe poster (under 60 KB, `fetchpriority=high`) the LCP element and fade the canvas in over it ([web.dev LCP](https://web.dev/articles/lcp)). Load three.js as a lazy chunk (about 160-176 KB gzip from PR bot snippets; unverified on r186). Default to WebGLRenderer, since WebGPURenderer auto-falls back to WebGL2 but is not free speed, and Spark is WebGL2-first ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGPURenderer.html), [Spark](https://github.com/sparkjsdev/spark)). Adapt resolution by measured frame time rather than detect-gpu alone, whose benchmark data stopped updating in December 2025 ([detect-gpu](https://github.com/pmndrs/detect-gpu)). Cache hashed assets as immutable via `_headers`; the Pages free plan caps files at 25 MiB and 20,000 per site ([Cloudflare limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)). Do not cache generated geometry, since seeded regeneration is deterministic; cap cached rooms, because Safari allows about 1 GB and evicts after 7 idle days ([web.dev storage](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web)) and 30 rooms at 25 MB is 750 MB worst case **(I)**. Same-document View Transitions suit globe-to-room-to-reader if wrapped in a no-op fallback ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions)). Bruno Simon's folio-2025 (MIT) is a usable reference for the KTX2 (`toktx --etc1s`), gltf-transform and instancing toolchain ([GitHub](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2025)).

## Conclusion

The notes change one assumption: "dynamic worlds" does not mean AI generating a world per visitor. It means generating the cheap part (geometry, shelves, books) from seeds on the visitor's device and baking the expensive part (light, atlases, splats) once, which is how both Roblox and Total Library actually get small. That makes the Long Room a template rather than a one-off: if Phase A hits its generation and lightmap targets, each later hall is mostly new parameters and a new evidence file.

The sharper insight is that evidence tags can be the product's signature game mechanic rather than a footnote. The same toggle that protects against hallucinated heritage ("show evidence only", ledger sorting, Living Page predictions) is what turns a model viewer into something a visitor plays with. The risk to manage is not rendering; it is that AI-made backdrops and a disputed scroll reading look more certain than they are.
