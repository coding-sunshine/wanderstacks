# Rebuild Wanderstacks as film, not software

**Rebuild Wanderstacks as a real-time three.js scene whose realism comes from art direction rather than from heavier technology: baked light, physically plausible materials, a photographic tone map, a small film-style post stack, a slow camera, opt-in sound, and an interface that disappears into the room.** The two reference sites you named do not support the "photoreal video or heavy tech" reading. iamtechartist.com/every-joule is a real-time three.js app with a deliberately stylised "3D ink drawing" look, about 390 KB of gzipped core JavaScript and no media files, so it teaches polish and loading, not photo textures. figmaformotion.com/v2/templates sells Figma design files, not code. The peer sites that actually read as cinematic (Igloo Inc, The Spark, Trionn, Rijksmuseum's Vermeer site) share a short list of habits that fit a static site with a roughly 5 MB first load. Historical accuracy needs its own system: a per-object certainty tier stored as data, a visible "sources and conjecture" note for each hall, and period facts enforced as rules (rolls in Roman niches, a flat ceiling in the Long Room before 1859, no cloth bindings before the 1820s). Much of the evidence is thin; notes below say which recommendations are sourced and which are my judgement.

## The references rely on restraint and art direction, not photoreal assets

I inspected the every-joule bundle directly. The WordPress page is a shell that iframes a Vite build with modulepreloaded chunks (`three`, `post`, and per-lesson chunks such as `lathe`, `spring` and `cutaway`), a branded inline-CSS loader with a progress bar, and a "needs graphics acceleration" fallback ([page](https://iamtechartist.com/every-joule/), [app](https://iamtechartist.com/Apps/every-joule/)). Measured by curl, `three-*.js` is 688 KB raw and 177 KB gzipped, post-processing is 151 KB gzipped, and the main chunk is 59 KB gzipped. String scans found EffectComposer, bloom, SMAA, ACES and AgX tone mapping, vignette and toon materials, and no GSAP, Lenis, GLTF loaders, KTX2 or video files. The geometry looks procedural. That matters to you: the "real" feel comes from tone mapping, bloom, anti-aliasing and a fast branded first paint over cheap geometry, the same budget logic Wanderstacks already follows. I did not run it in a browser, so frame rate and mobile behaviour are unverified.

The Figma for Motion catalogue is a paid marketplace of "50+" website concepts (Medieval Armor, Frost, Torii gate and similar). Premium costs $59.90 and includes Figma source files plus prompts for turning them into sites; the site itself is Next.js with lazy-playing video previews ([templates](https://figmaformotion.com/v2/templates), [home](https://figmaformotion.com/v2/)). Its genre is one hero object, big type and scroll chapters. Use it as a storyboard reference only; there is no real-time 3D technique to reuse, and the earlier research also judged the templates not worth buying for this build.

The peers carry the real lessons. Igloo Inc (Awwwards Site of the Year 2024) is real-time three.js with custom geometry exporters, staged texture loading and background shader compilation, tested on low-end devices ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). The Spark, a "short film that runs in a browser", gets depth by projecting painted imagery onto simple shapes, softens aliasing with a CRT-style pass, loads one scene at a time, and ties footstep sound to scroll speed ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/01/09/the-spark-engineering-an-immersive-story-first-web-experience/)). Trionn fakes glow with layered lines instead of a bloom pass, fakes depth from a depth map on a single plane, synthesises its sound with Web Audio instead of shipping files, and precompiles shaders in idle time ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/15/the-architecture-behind-trionn-coordinating-gsap-three-js-lenis-and-web-audio/)). Rijksmuseum's "Closer to Johannes Vermeer" is the best match for your subject: its interface "quickly fades into the background", it pairs 5-micron imagery with narrated tours, and it logged a 12-minute average session ([Q42](https://q42.nl/en/work/johannes-vermeer)). Getty and Resn's "Sculpting Harmony" (Awwwards Site of the Month, November 2023) drives a choreographed aerial camera with scroll and treats type as an animated object ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sculpting-harmony-wins-site-of-the-month-november-2023.html)). The common thread is that the artefact or camera path is the interface.

## Real-time three.js with baked light is the best realism per megabyte

Pre-rendered footage is the only route where path-traced light, glossy leather and window god-rays come free, but scroll-scrubbing needs all-intra encoding, which one practitioner measured at 8 MB (H.264) or 13 MB (VP9) per 1280 px, 30 fps clip against 1-2 MB for a normal encode ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix)). That alone breaks a 5 MB first load and fixes the camera path. AI-generated establishing shots carry a worse problem for a site about real buildings: windows shift and layouts change between clips, and licence terms vary by tool and tier ([Hailuo](https://hailuoai.video/knowledge/architectural-realism-virtual-backlots-ai-prompting), [licenseorg](https://licenseorg.com/blog/ai-video-licensing-2026)). They would present invented details as fact, so keep them out of any factual shot. Gaussian splats photograph a real room, but Spark 2.0 budgets about 500K splats on mobile and the earlier research found no library whose capture you can legally self-host ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/world-labs-releases-spark-v2.0)); SOG compression cut one 4M-splat scene from 1 GB to 42 MB ([PlayCanvas](https://blog.playcanvas.com/playcanvas-open-sources-sog-format-for-gaussian-splatting/)). Both belong in lazy, optional chapters after first load.

The base route is therefore real-time three.js, which also keeps the book interactive. The recipe, with sourced parts first: pick the tone mapper deliberately, since AgX is less saturated than ACES and Khronos PBR Neutral aims at true-to-life colour ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/tone-mapping-overview/75204)), which I judge safer for wood and leather hue. Use CC0 PBR materials from Poly Haven (780+ textures, 980+ HDRIs) and ambientCG at 1K, compressed to KTX2 for roughly 4-8x less GPU memory ([Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/license), [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-textures-hdris-materials)). Add a post stack from pmndrs/postprocessing, which offers bloom, depth of field, vignette, god rays, grain, chromatic aberration and LUT grading merged into few passes ([README](https://github.com/pmndrs/postprocessing)). Bake lighting as the earlier report recommends, with path-traced lightmaps from three-gpu-pathtracer in daylight and candlelight variants, proven first on the Long Room because the evidence is one demo and one tutorial. From my own judgement rather than a source: the common CG tells are tiling textures, missing contact shadows and bounce light, perfectly straight shelves, dust-free air, over-sharp no-depth-of-field images, and wrong scale cues, and each has a cheap fix (roughness and normal variation, decals for wear, baked AO, window bloom, a faint dust-mote layer, slightly irregular book heights).

WebGPU is an upgrade, not a dependency. three.js has an automatic WebGL2 fallback and r183 renamed `PostProcessing` to `RenderPipeline` ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-03-18-threejs-r183-render-pipeline)), but no source gave frame rates for post stacks on mid-range phones. Ship a mobile tier: cap device pixel ratio (about 1.5), drop depth of field and volumetrics, and switch tiers with `gsap.matchMedia()` as Trionn does. Hybrid depth-composited video plus 3D is feasible but I found no case study, and matching grain and exposure between plate and foreground is where it fails, so I would not start there.

## The interface should vanish into the room

The sourced pattern is consistent: National Gallery hides its nav behind a discreet hamburger so imagery leads, and Van Gogh Museum tints its dropdowns to the page colour ([Numiko](https://numiko.com/insights/best-museum-and-gallery-websites-2026)). Sameness in AI-built sites comes from median defaults such as Inter everywhere, indigo gradients, glass panels and three-card rows ([925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)). No source ties gold-on-black, micro-cap label rows or decorative frames specifically to that problem; that link is an inference from your own brief, but the museum evidence supports the fix. The rules I recommend: one small control cluster (sound toggle plus map glyph) at low opacity until hover; information in the world through spine labels, plaques and lectern placards that appear on proximity and fade; one lower-third caption per room entry (room, city, year, about 4-6 seconds); plain captions with a soft text shadow instead of panels; a chapter card per library that fades and disappears; and an accent colour sampled from each room's own light, such as lamp amber or window blue, rather than a global gold. Letterbox bars should appear only as a transition state. Avoid glass pills, floating nav over the scene, parchment textures behind UI, and ornamental frames. These specifics are my design judgement, though the diegetic-UI principle (information lives in the world) is established game-UI practice ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/diegetic-interfaces-game-design)).

Loading should be the first shot. Follow every-joule: inline the loader CSS so first paint is already branded, show one line of text and a hairline progress bar, reveal the globe the moment it renders, and stream the rest, with shader precompile in idle time as Igloo and Trionn do. The Spark loads one scene at a time with short interstitial loaders, which suits one-library-at-a-time loading.

**Typography** should use three roles and four to five families at most, self-hosted and subset: one neutral face for UI and captions, one humanist serif with real italics for book reading (EB Garamond or Spectral, the latter designed screen-first, [Google Design](https://design.google/library/spectral-new-screen-first-typeface/)), and era-specific display faces used only on chapter cards and in-world signage. That matches film practice, where The Age of Innocence uses period Bodoni italic and Bickham-style script for titles only ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/32000/the-age-of-innocence-1993-title-sequence)). Available OFL revivals include Cinzel (Roman capitals), Uncial Antiqua, Grenze Gotisch and Pirata One (blackletter), Cormorant, and IM Fell ([Notebook & Penguin](https://www.notebookandpenguin.com/old-english-blackletter-gothic-google-fonts/), [Cormorant](https://github.com/CatharsisFonts/Cormorant)). The era mapping is my typographic judgement and unverified for licence and glyph coverage: Cinzel for Celsus, Cormorant for Renaissance halls, Caslon or Baskerville revivals for Baroque and Enlightenment, a Didone for Labrouste and the Library of Congress, and per-script Noto families for non-Latin libraries instead of faking them with Latin "exotic" fonts. No faithful free Carolingian face turned up; substitute and say so. Do not use blackletter outside the German-speaking world.

**Motion** splits by mode. Lenis plus GSAP ScrollTrigger is the standard scroll stack (drive Lenis from the GSAP ticker, per secondary sources to re-check against the Lenis docs), but Wanderstacks is a walkable scene, so scroll choreography pays off only in the globe-to-library intro and reading panes. In-room moves are better as short eased tweens; the earlier report recommended gliding to any floor point over 0.6-1.2 seconds with head bob off by default, grounded in Xbox accessibility guidance ([XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)). Same-document View Transitions now work in Chrome and Edge 111+, Safari 18+ and Firefox 144+ ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions)), which covers the dissolve for `prefers-reduced-motion`, where large-object travel and parallax can trigger vestibular discomfort ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)). Replace flights with a fade through black and keep text and sound.

**Sound** must be opt-in because browsers block AudioContext until a gesture ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/web-audio-autoplay)) and autoplay is an accessibility failure ([A11y Project](https://a11yproject.com/posts/never-use-auto-play)). Make the Enter tap the gate ("Enter" or "Enter with sound"), keep a persistent mute control, load audio only after opt-in, and make the experience complete without it ([A Short Journey](https://ashortjourney-project.pages.dev/notes/sound-without-autoplay/)). Layers I would build: crossfaded room tone per library, surface-keyed footsteps (stone, wood, carpet) scaled to walking speed as The Spark does, page-turn foley, and one low swell on chapter cards. Synthesised or tiny samples keep the byte cost near zero, as Trionn shows. OpenAIR has Creative Commons impulse responses for heritage spaces but none for a library hall, so label any convolution reverb "proxy acoustic, not this room" ([OpenAIR](https://www.openair.hosted.york.ac.uk)).

## Accuracy needs sources, rules and a visible uncertainty layer

The professional standards give the frame. The London Charter has six principles (implementation, aims and methods, research sources, documentation, sustainability, access) and requires sources to be "identified and evaluated in a structured and documented way" ([London Charter](https://www.londoncharter.org/principles.html)). The Seville Principles extend it for virtual archaeology, with a compliance seal separating source-based reconstruction from speculation ([Indiana VWHL](https://vwhl.luddy.indiana.edu/sevillePrinciples/index.html)). Uncertainty is usually shown by colour, opacity or line sharpness per source confidence, with no single standard ([arXiv 2503.11653](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11653v1)). I did not fetch the full Charter text on paradata or the exact eight Seville principles, so read those before claiming compliance. The practical scheme, my proposal: give every object a certainty tier (attested, inferred from a parallel, conjectural) stored as data; keep the cinematic grade on by default; offer an opt-in "show evidence" toggle that desaturates or dithers conjectural items; and add a short "sources and conjecture" panel per hall. Ubisoft's candour is the model: it admitted that for the House of Wisdom "we didn't really know where it was exactly or even the shape of it" ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/assassins-creed-mirage-lost-city)), and credited named historians for the Discovery Tour ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/10/07/assassins-creed-mirage-interview-ubisoft-montreal-raphael-weyland-historian-interview/)). Never generate text, faces or inscriptions with AI, and badge any AI-assisted asset with model and date.

Period rules catch the errors that make a room feel fake. Verified facts from the notes: Roman papyrus rolls were normally 25-33 cm tall with a rod (umbilicus) and a hanging tag, stored in niches or cylindrical capsae, and the codex replaced the roll by the 4th century ([Encyclopaedia Romana](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/encyclopaedia_romana/scroll/scrollcodex.html), [historyofinformation.com](https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=2677)). The Library of Celsus had tiered niches averaging 2.55 x 1.1 x 0.58 m holding about 12,000 scrolls ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Celsus)), so fill them with rolls, not codices. Chained books sat fore-edge out with titles written on the fore-edge and a chain on a rod, as at Hereford ([Chained library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_library), [British Library](https://britishlibrary.typepad.co.uk/digitisedmanuscripts/2015/02/written-on-the-edge.html)), so uniform modern spines are wrong for medieval scenes. The Long Room was flat-ceilinged until the barrel vault was completed in May 1861 ([TCD](https://www.tcd.ie/library/exhibitions/choicebooks/newlibrary.php)), so a pre-1860 view must lose the vault, which also supports a phase slider. Publisher's cloth binding appears in the 1820s with disputed credit ([bookbinding.com](https://bookbinding.com/origins-of-cloth-bindings/), [Ligatus](https://www.ligatus.org.uk/node/550)), so earlier books are leather, vellum or paper boards. Sainte-Geneviève (1843-50) has exposed iron and early gas lighting ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Henri-Labrouste)). Strahov's Philosophical Hall has a Maulbertsch ceiling of 1794 over carved walnut shelving ([Strahov](https://www.strahovskyklaster.cz/en/architecture/library-halls)); Admont dates to 1776 with Altomonte frescoes ([Stift Admont](https://stiftadmont.at/en/about-the-abbey-library/)). Treat Pergamon and Alexandria interiors as conjectural.

Several things the notes could not verify, so do not assert them: Admont's actual binding colours, why candles were barred in great halls, papyrus and aged-leather colour values, Timbuktu storage details, Nalanda's physical fabric, and the Notre-Dame and Alexandria digital-twin precedents. Source each as it enters the build, using Commons photographs, the institutions' own pages and scholarly sources, and record the citation next to the object. Two practical consequences: a "too clean" room is itself an inaccuracy, but wear must be evidence-based and wear values are unsourced, so treat them as art direction labelled as such.

## Conclusion

The reference sites changed the question. Their cinematic quality does not come from photoreal assets or video, so the rebuild is mostly a discipline of subtraction: cheap procedural geometry, baked light, one tone-mapped and slightly imperfect image, a slow camera, and an interface that appears only when the room has something to say. That discipline also protects the 5 MB budget and makes the hardest problem, believable historical content, easier, because every added effect is cheaper than one more fabricated detail.

The less obvious implication is that accuracy can be a cinematic device rather than a disclaimer. A Long Room that loses its vault when you scrub back to 1850, or a Celsus niche stocked with rolls, makes the viewer feel the evidence, and a visible conjecture toggle turns uncertainty into the kind of candour that earned Vermeer and Discovery Tour their credibility. Two gaps remain: no measured mobile frame rates for any recommended stack, and no verified mapping of era typefaces to licences and glyph coverage. Prove the full recipe on the Long Room on a mid-range phone before extending it to other halls.
