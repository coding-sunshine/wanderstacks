# Rebuild WorldLibs around plates and one book

**WorldLibs should stop being a procedurally generated 3D site with a nicer skin and become a photographed film you can step into.** The two references you named get their beauty from different places, and the rebuild needs both. figmaformotion.com/v2/templates is built from photoreal pre-rendered plates (generated stills and video served as webp and webm, not real-time 3D) with restrained display type layered inside the scene's depth, so a headline can sit partly behind foreground moss (coordinator browser session, 2026-11-10). iamtechartist.com/every-joule gets its beauty from a diegetic object: an open hardback lying under warm window light is the interface, with hatched, drawn 3D objects standing on the page (coordinator browser session, 2026-11-10). For WorldLibs that means chapters built from offline path-traced renders, public-domain photography and, only where honest, generated video, with real-time three.js reserved for a few walkable signature moments. The DOM book reader becomes the navigation spine, as the book is in every-joule. The 2026 certainty scheme from Unger et al. becomes a visible design feature: photoreal marks attested fabric, a translucent ghost marks the hypothetical, and clay or sketch-shaded rendering marks interpretation ([Internet Archaeology 72](https://intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue72/13/full-text.html)). That rule also tells you which of your 52 halls may look photographic at all. Three constraints decide the plan: the 5 MB arrival budget survives only if plates load lazily by chapter, no open scans exist for the Long Room, Admont or Strahov interiors, and several key sources were blocked, so some numbers below are my engineering estimates and are marked as such.

## What the two references teach, and what the earlier report got wrong

The earlier report on this project read both references through bundle inspection and concluded that realism comes from restraint, tone mapping and cheap geometry. Direct observation changes half of that. The coordinator's real-browser session shows that figmaformotion's 56 templates are led by photoreal generated media: each card carries tool chips (Figma, Midjourney, Flow/Google Veo, CapCut) and the heroes are stills and video, not WebGL. The craft is compositional. Giant display type is layered between planes of the image, so the word "Secret" on the Torii template is partly hidden by foreground moss. A four-colour palette sampled from the image appears as chips beside each template. The chrome is hairline tiny-caps navigation, glass thumbnail cards with play buttons, a circular rotating-text scroll badge, strong depth of field and dusk light. Fonts are chosen per template, for example Viaoda Libre with Imprima for "Step into Wonder" and Ginto Nord with Onest for "Secret Torii". The site's own chrome is lavender with condensed black display type and a lime call to action. The earlier report priced the Premium tier at $59.90; the session saw $44.95 on sale against $89.90 normally, with client use allowed and resale not, so use the session figure. The author is Daniel Snows, and the earlier notes confirm the product is Figma source files with prompts for turning them into sites ([figmaformotion.com](https://figmaformotion.com/)).

every-joule is where the earlier research and the session agree and then diverge. The curl inspection found a Vite build with three.js, bloom, SMAA, AgX and toon materials, about 390 KB of gzipped core JavaScript and no media files, framed inside a WordPress shell ([earlier teardown of the app](https://iamtechartist.com/Apps/every-joule/)). That explains why the DOM probe finds no canvas on the page itself. What the earlier work could not see is the composition: a photoreal-feeling desk diorama with soft cast shadows and warm window light, a left page of real editorial typography (serif italic, equations, a keyword glossary, checkboxes, a "lift the flap" prediction card with a handwritten arrow), and one floating glass pill at the bottom holding menu, previous, play, chapter number and title, next, mute, a contrast toggle and an education toggle (coordinator browser session, 2026-11-10). The hand-drawn objects sit inside a photographically lit scene, and the contrast is the point. For WorldLibs the transfer is direct. The book is the interface, and a non-photoreal drawn language can mark reconstructed content inside a photoreal room, which is exactly what the certainty tiers need.

The earlier report also advised against glass pills and floating chrome. The reference proves one restrained control strip works when everything else lives in the object, so the rule becomes a single strip, kept small, and built as a bookmark ribbon or hairline bar instead of a frosted panel. That is my design judgement, not a sourced finding.

Peer award sites confirm the production logic. Their premium read is mostly pre-authored: Lusion's case study describes Houdini cloth baked into textures and Redshift renders shipped as video with the lighting extracted, with runtime kept cheap ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/case-study-for-lusion-by-lusion-winner-of-site-of-the-month-may.html)). Immersive Garden's Cartier Watches & Wonders build uses six self-contained 3D alcoves with scroll-driven room transitions and a Web Audio layer, which is the closest analogue to WorldLibs rooms ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)). Igloo Inc won Site of the Year 2024 with real-time three.js, chromatic aberration and frost in transitions, and staged loading tested on low-end devices ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). The Spark builds depth by projecting painted imagery onto simple shapes ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/01/09/the-spark-engineering-an-immersive-story-first-web-experience/)), and Trionn fakes depth from a depth map on a single plane ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/15/the-architecture-behind-trionn-coordinating-gsap-three-js-lenis-and-web-audio/)). The common pattern is authored imagery first and real-time only where interaction demands it.

## Experience architecture: five acts, with the book as the spine

The structure below is my proposal, built on three sourced patterns: guided-first with optional free roam (Discovery Tour, Smithsonian Voyager tours, and the New York Times' restricted camera in its 2023 Notre-Dame piece), real object before room (the Book of Kells Experience shows the manuscript before the Long Room), and Voyager's annotation, article and tour triad ([Voyager docs](https://smithsonian.github.io/dpo-voyager/explorer/overview), [Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/dublin-book-kells-manuscript-immersive-experience-2384605), [Storybench](https://www.storybench.org/?p=22115)).

Act one is the night-earth globe, kept as real-time because it already works and because a lit planet is a cheap, strong first shot. The loader is the first frame: inline CSS, one line of text, a hairline progress bar, as every-joule does. Act two is the book opening. The camera descends from the globe to a desk under window light, a closed hardback on it, and the visitor opens it. This is the diegetic hinge: the book's contents page is the catalogue of 52 libraries, its ribbon is the control strip, and its pages carry the editorial typography, glossary and "what we know, what we guessed" notes. The existing DOM book reader becomes this object rather than a modal on top of the scene. Act three is a library chapter, a scroll-driven sequence of three to five photoreal plates with title-in-depth, a lower-third giving place and year, and one paradata line. Act four is the walkable moment, offered on a button labelled "Step inside", available for the five bespoke rooms only, with click-to-move gliding and no head bob by default. Act five is the reading: pulling a book from a shelf returns to the desk and the page. Entering and leaving a room is a cross-dissolve, not a flight, which also solves reduced motion.

This changes the split between plates and real-time. The five bespoke rooms get both a plate chapter and a walkable room. The other 47 halls keep their procedural real-time hall as the only room, and they appear in the book with a drawn, sketch-shaded plate in the interpretation style, because nothing licensable supports a photographic claim for them. That is honest and cheap. It also means the rebuild's photoreal budget concentrates on five rooms instead of spreading thin.

The certainty tiers decide the rendering style per element. Attested structure renders photographic, hypothetical structure renders as a translucent ghost, and interpretation renders clay or sketch-shaded ([Internet Archaeology 72](https://intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue72/13/full-text.html)). Applied to the five rooms, the Celsus façade is attested because it was re-erected by anastylosis in 1970 to 1978, while its interior is lost and must be clay or ghost ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Celsus)). The Long Room's barrel vault was only completed in May 1861, so a pre-1860 view must show a flat ceiling in ghost state, which turns the accuracy rule into a time slider ([TCD](https://www.tcd.ie/library/exhibitions/choicebooks/newlibrary.php)). Admont (1776) and Strahov's Philosophical Hall (ceiling 1794) are extant and can be photographic, with the books themselves tagged separately as photographed, transcribed or placeholder ([Stift Admont](https://stiftadmont.at/en/about-the-abbey-library/), [Strahov](https://www.strahovskyklaster.cz/en/architecture/library-halls)). Alexandria, Pergamon, Nineveh and Nalanda have no extant interior and should never appear photographic. One tension needs an explicit decision: a path-traced render of an extant room is photographic in look but still a reconstruction from your own model, so label it "rendered from measured sources" in the paradata drawer rather than letting it pass as a photograph.

## The visual and motion system

Type does the cinematic work, so the pairing is a core decision rather than decoration. The figmaformotion fonts (Viaoda Libre, Ginto Nord) are a per-template choice, and Ginto Nord is a commercial face, so WorldLibs needs an open equivalent system. The notes list Google Fonts families under the SIL Open Font Licence, though the individual licences were not re-verified and each family's page should be checked before shipping. My proposed roles: a high-contrast display serif for hero titles, Cormorant Garamond at light weights and large sizes because its hairlines fail below about 24 px; Cinzel for Roman-era chapters only, in tracked capitals; EB Garamond or Spectral for book reading, the latter designed screen-first ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/spectral-new-screen-first-typeface/)); one neutral sans such as Hanken Grotesk for the control strip and captions; and IM Fell only for archival spine and plaque details. Non-Latin libraries get Noto families per script instead of Latin faces faking an exotic look. No faithful free Carolingian face turned up, so substitute and say so. Blackletter stays out except for German-speaking halls. Display titles run roughly `clamp(2.5rem, 7vw + 1rem, 8rem)` with tight tracking and a line height near 1, with a hairline rule and a small tracked date line beneath; these values are conventions from my notes, not measured from the references.

Title-in-depth is the signature technique, and it needs a production step the earlier plan lacked. Each hero plate is delivered as at least two layers: a background plate and a foreground matte with alpha (moss, a lectern edge, a ladder rail, a hanging lamp). The headline lives in the DOM between them, so the foreground covers part of a letter exactly as "Secret" is covered on the Torii template. For path-traced renders this comes from rendering a foreground pass and a Z-depth pass from the same camera in Blender; for photographs it means hand-cutting the matte. This is my engineering proposal. The cheap parallax variant, a depth map displacing a single plane, is documented at Trionn and fits the budget ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/15/the-architecture-behind-trionn-coordinating-gsap-three-js-lenis-and-web-audio/)).

Palette follows the figmaformotion method: sample four colours from each chapter's plate and publish them as chips in the book's margin, which doubles as an accent for that chapter's UI. That replaces a global gold, which the earlier report already identified as a generic tell. Provisional directions, to be confirmed against the actual plates: Long Room in aged oak and low amber, Admont in cream stucco with pastel gilding (the pale-binding detail is unconfirmed), Strahov in dark carved walnut with gilt, the Library of Congress in marble and green-gold, Celsus in sun-bleached limestone at dusk. Grade with AgX or Khronos PBR Neutral, which the three.js community describes as less saturated than ACES and true-to-life respectively ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/tone-mapping-overview/75204)). Add grain as a static overlay at 4 to 8 percent rather than an animated filter, a light vignette, and a 2.39:1 letterbox that appears only during cinematic transitions. Those grain and letterbox values are unsourced conventions to tune by eye.

The motion stack is mostly free and current. GSAP 3.13 made the whole toolset free, including a rewritten SplitText that is about 50 percent smaller, supports screen readers and re-splits responsively ([GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/)). Check the Standard licence terms before shipping, since the notes did not verify them. Lenis is MIT, wraps native scroll so sticky and anchors keep working, and disables smoothing under `prefers-reduced-motion` ([Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). Use Lenis and ScrollTrigger only on the scrolling HTML chapters and the book, never in the walkable view, and drive one shared requestAnimationFrame loop from the GSAP ticker for GSAP, Lenis and three.js. CSS scroll-driven animations are now in Chrome, Safari 26 and Firefox 159 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2026)), so simple progress bars and parallax can go native with GSAP as fallback. Same-document View Transitions suit the room dissolves; the notes disagree on whether Firefox support begins at 133 or 144, so feature-detect instead of assuming ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions)). Easing and duration tokens, all unverified conventions: expo-out `0.16, 1, 0.3, 1` for headline reveals of 700 to 1000 ms, quint-out `0.22, 1, 0.36, 1` for image scale-in from 1.08 over about 1.6 s, cubic in-out for camera-like moves, and room dissolves of 800 to 1200 ms. Stagger by line at 80 to 120 ms for anything read, and by letter only for short display words. Under reduced motion, use `gsap.matchMedia()` to swap travel, blur, Ken Burns and letterbox slides for 200 to 300 ms opacity fades, and offer an in-app toggle as well ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)).

Sound is opt-in, gated by the Enter gesture, because browsers block AudioContext until a user action ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/web-audio-autoplay)). Layers: crossfaded room tone per library, surface-keyed footsteps, page-turn foley and one low swell on chapter titles, all lazy-loaded after opt-in. Trionn synthesises most of its sound with Web Audio to avoid shipping files, which is the right byte discipline here.

## Asset pipeline per bespoke room, and who owns what

The binding fact from the asset research is that almost nothing open exists at room scale. The one verified open 3D asset for any of the five is an exterior Celsus model, and it is the wrong kind. The pipeline therefore starts from your own measured modelling, rendered offline, with texture and reference material from sources whose licences are checked per file.

| Room | Realistic source for plates | Licensing status | What stays unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long Room, Dublin | Own model from measured dimensions, Poly Haven and ambientCG CC0 materials, rendered path-traced | No Long Room scan found on Sketchfab. Trinity permits non-flash personal photography only; reuse needs a request and the Book of Kells is excluded ([TCD](https://www.tcd.ie/library/dris/copyright.php)) | Irish freedom-of-panorama position unverified; no source for pre-1861 flat-ceiling appearance beyond the date |
| Admont | Own model, plus Commons photographs for reference where the licence allows | Austrian freedom of panorama covers architecture of public interiors, not statues, books or frescoes; official photos by Stefan Leitner are all rights reserved ([Commons](https://c.enwp.org/wiki/Commons:CRT/Austria), [Stift Admont](https://stiftadmont.at/en/architektur/)) | Commons file counts and licences not checked; actual binding colours unconfirmed |
| Strahov | Own model from published dimensions and Commons references | Czech freedom of panorama and the abbey's photography policy not verified | Nothing sourced on shelving finish beyond carved walnut; no open scan |
| Library of Congress | HABS/HAER drawings and period photographs from loc.gov as measured reference and texture | HABS material is generally public domain with a rights statement per record ([LoC](https://www.loc.gov/collections/historic-american-buildings-landscapes-and-engineering-records/about-this-collection/)) | The Jefferson Building record number and drawing count were not verified (403) |
| Celsus, Ephesus | Facade: public photographs or a decimated crowdsourced model; interior: clay or ghost render | Sketchfab "Library of Celsus - Crowdsourced photogrammetry" is CC-BY 4.0, 1.1M triangles, built from 200 public photos, and flagged NoAI ([Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/library-of-celsus-crowdsourced-photogrammetry-1cc6ad6af508422398f60f902cb70e88)). Trimble's 46-scan point cloud exists but is not stated to be open ([Trimble](https://www.trimble.com/en/blog/construction/article/how-trimble-used-point-cloud-scanner-to-create-most-detailed-model-ever-of-ancient-library)) | The NoAI tag means keeping that mesh out of any generative pipeline; 1.1M triangles needs decimation |

Three rules follow. Stick to CC0, CC-BY and public-domain inputs, because a personal-use institutional policy does not authorise publication and a non-commercial licence would block any monetised version (my reading; not legal advice). Record every input's licence beside the asset in the paradata data. Keep generated imagery away from factual shots: AI video drifts, with windows shifting and layouts changing between clips, and licence terms vary by tool and tier ([Hailuo](https://hailuoai.video/knowledge/architectural-realism-virtual-backlots-ai-prompting), [licenseorg](https://licenseorg.com/blog/ai-video-licensing-2026)). Its legitimate use here is atmosphere with no architectural claim, such as dust in a light shaft or a candle macro, badged with model and date. The primary terms pages for Google, OpenAI, Kuaishou, Runway and Midjourney were not fetched, so verify each before using any output. Gaussian splats are the strongest "real" read, and Spark 2.0 budgets about 500,000 splats on mobile ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/world-labs-releases-spark-v2.0)), but SOG compression took one 4M-splat scene from 1 GB to 42 MB ([PlayCanvas](https://blog.playcanvas.com/playcanvas-open-sources-sog-format-for-gaussian-splatting/)), and no capture of these halls exists to legally host. Skip them unless a partner captures a room.

Plates ship as lazy chapter packs. Pre-rendered scroll-scrubbed video is the costly case: one practitioner measured about 8 MB (H.264) or 13 MB (VP9) for a 1280 px, 30 fps all-intra clip against 1 to 2 MB for a normal encode ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix)). So default to stills with depth layers and short, normally encoded loops that play on visibility, as figmaformotion's own cards do, and reserve all-intra scrubbing for one hero sequence per bespoke room, loaded after entry. An equirectangular or wide still in AVIF or WebP is an estimated 300 to 800 KB at 4096 px, which is my estimate, not a measurement.

## Accuracy as a visible layer, not a disclaimer

The London Charter requires research sources to be "identified and evaluated in a structured and documented way", and the Seville Principles add a compliance seal distinguishing source-based reconstruction from speculation ([London Charter](https://www.londoncharter.org/principles.html), [Indiana VWHL](https://vwhl.luddy.indiana.edu/sevillePrinciples/index.html)). The Charter's paradata principle says the relationship between sources, implicit knowledge, explicit reasoning and visual outcomes should be understandable ([London Charter](https://www.londoncharter.org/introduction.html)). Field critique warns that blending evidence-based and inferred elements produces unwarranted certainty ([Dublin Core paper](https://dcpapers.dublincore.org/article/952627904)). WorldLibs can answer all three with a data model, not a footnote.

Store a certainty tier on every object: surviving, documented or conjectural, plus a source list, a licence and a one-line reasoning note. The renderer reads the tier. Surviving objects are photographic, documented objects get a faint desaturation and a cited note, and conjectural objects render as clay or sketch-shaded, with movable artefacts and vegetation more transparent still, following the Unger scheme. A per-room "what we know, what we guessed" panel sits on the book page, and a paradata drawer lists sources and decisions. Candour is a proven trust device: Ubisoft admitted of the House of Wisdom that "we didn't really know where it was exactly or even the shape of it" ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/games/assassins-creed-mirage-lost-city)), and Discovery Tour names its historians. The Rijksmuseum's Operation Night Watch shows that the process itself is content, so a "how this room was built" layer is worth a page ([Frieze](https://frieze.com/article/rembrandts-night-watch-will-be-restored-full-public-view)).

Period rules become automated checks on the data. Roman rolls were normally 25 to 33 cm tall on a rod with a hanging tag and sat in niches or cylindrical capsae, and Celsus' niches averaged 2.55 by 1.1 by 0.58 m and held about 12,000 scrolls, so fill them with rolls, never codices ([Encyclopaedia Romana](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/encyclopaedia_romana/scroll/scrollcodex.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Celsus)). Chained medieval books sat fore-edge out with titles written on the fore-edge, so uniform modern spines fail there ([Chained library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chained_library), [British Library](https://britishlibrary.typepad.co.uk/digitisedmanuscripts/2015/02/written-on-the-edge.html)). Publisher's cloth binding appears in the 1820s, so earlier books are leather, vellum or boards ([bookbinding.com](https://bookbinding.com/origins-of-cloth-bindings/)). Sainte-Geneviève (1843 to 1850) carries exposed iron and early gas lighting ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Henri-Labrouste)). Never generate text, faces or inscriptions with AI. Wear is art direction and should be labelled as such, since no source supplies evidence-based wear values.

## Budgets for performance, mobile and access

Keep the existing 5 MB arrival budget for the globe, loader, type subset and book shell. Everything else loads on demand. My proposed ceilings, all engineering judgement to be replaced by measurement: about 3 MB per chapter plate pack, a separately gated walkable room download, and audio only after opt-in. Self-host and subset fonts, preload only the display face, and precompile shaders in idle time as Igloo and Trionn do. KTX2 compression cuts GPU texture memory roughly 4 to 8 times ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-textures-hdris-materials)), and Draco decoding can slow first load on weak devices, so measure before adopting it ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/an-open-world-space-experience-as-a-portfolio/74951.md)). Cap device pixel ratio near 1.5 on phones, drop depth of field and volumetrics, and switch tiers with `gsap.matchMedia()`. Cap any decoded image-sequence cache, since 200 frames at 2000 px can exceed 400 MB decoded. WebGPU is an upgrade, not a dependency: three.js falls back to WebGL2 automatically ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide)), and no source gave frame rates for mid-range phones.

Accessibility is where award sites lose points, and the research found published scores to prove it: Lacoste Polo Factory scored 9.20 for animation and 6.00 for accessibility ([ZURB Radar](https://radar.zurb.com/article/your-award-winning-site-might-be-locking-people-out)). Scrolljacking breaks touch, keyboard and trackpad behaviour ([Web Designer Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/how-scrolljacking-breaks-ux-fundamentals/)). So keep native scroll, make every chapter reachable as plain HTML text with the book's keyboard-navigable contents list, provide a text-first reading mode, ship reduced-motion and contrast toggles (every-joule exposes both in its control strip), and follow The Pudding's mobile rules: short steps, pixel heights from `window.innerHeight` instead of `vh`, a static fallback, no hover dependence ([The Pudding](https://pudding.cool/process/responsive-scrollytelling/)). Offer click-to-move gliding with no head bob and a field-of-view control, because teleport-style movement reduces sickness ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-05819-7_13)). Captions accompany every sound.

## A phased from-scratch build

Treat the current codebase as a quarry, not a base. The room textures, book texts (134 built books) and `libs.json` carry over; the shell, UI and chapter system are new. Agency estimates for this class of site run 8 to 14 weeks for a standard build and 5 to 9 months for flagship worlds ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)), a marketing figure but a useful warning against promising a quick pass.

| Phase | Scope | Exit test |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Freeze the language | Tokens (type, palette method, easing), certainty schema, budgets; the companion prototype validates the direction on one chapter | Direction approved against the prototype; schema covers all five rooms |
| 1. New shell | Loader, globe act, book-as-navigation, strip, GSAP/Lenis loop, reduced-motion paths, text-first mode | Arrival under 5 MB on throttled mobile; every chapter reachable by keyboard |
| 2. One chapter end to end | Long Room plate pack, title-in-depth, lower-third, paradata drawer, 1860 slider | A tester can tell attested from conjectural without being told |
| 3. First walkable moment | Long Room baked-light room, click-to-move, sound | Acceptable frame rate on a mid-range phone with the mobile tier |
| 4. Other four rooms | Admont, Strahov, Library of Congress, Celsus in the same pipeline | Each room has a licence-checked source list |
| 5. The other 47 halls | Procedural halls plus sketch-shaded book plates, tier data per hall | No hall appears photographic without a source |
| 6. Hardening | Accessibility audit, budget audit, paradata review, real-device tests | Budgets met on measured devices, not estimates |

Prove phases 2 and 3 on the Long Room before touching the others, since the baked-lighting route rests on one demo and one tutorial in the notes, and the three-gpu-pathtracer workflow was not verified at production scale.

## Risks and gaps that should shape the decision

The research hit real limits. Several primary sources returned HTTP 403 (Codrops' Lusion and Bruno articles, Art of the Title, Awwwards 2025 pages), so peer-site claims rest on search snippets and published case studies. No measured transfer weights exist for Igloo, Lusion, The Spark or Trionn, and the only hard code sizes are for every-joule's bundle. The motion and typography values above are conventions from prior knowledge, flagged as unverified in the source notes, so tune them by eye. Title-sequence references, sound loudness targets and per-family font licences were not verified. Sound design has no sourced guidance beyond autoplay policy.

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| No open scans for Long Room, Admont, Strahov | "Real-looking" has no photographic ground truth | Render from measured models, label as such, seek partner captures or commissioned photography |
| Photoreal render of an extant room reads as a photograph | Unger warns photorealism implies certainty | Paradata label "rendered from measured sources"; per-object tiers |
| Plate weight breaks the 5 MB budget | Fails the existing arrival rule | Lazy chapter packs, stills first, one all-intra sequence per room after entry |
| Interior photo licensing (TCD personal-use, Admont rights-reserved) | Legal exposure | CC0, CC-BY or public-domain inputs only; log every licence; not legal advice |
| AI-generated media misrepresents real buildings | Invented details presented as fact | Atmosphere only, badged, never on a factual shot |
| Title-in-depth needs matte production per plate | Larger art workload than the old plan | Standardise a Blender foreground and Z pass; depth-map fallback |
| No mobile frame-rate data for the stack | Mobile tier is unproven | Real-device tests at phase 3 before extending |
| Unverified numbers (budgets, easings, grain, fonts, estimates) | Look authoritative but are not | Treated as proposals; replace with measurements in phases 1 to 3 |

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is that the two references solve different problems, and WorldLibs has both. figmaformotion shows how to make a still image feel like a place by putting type inside its depth; every-joule shows how to make an interface feel like an object by making it the book. The earlier report's instinct that realism comes from restraint holds for real-time rooms, but it undersold the plate route: once plates load lazily and only five rooms get the full treatment, photoreal chapters become affordable, and the certainty tiers tell you where photographic looks are allowed.

The less obvious consequence is that honesty becomes the visual identity. Most heritage sites hide what they invented; a site that renders the lost interior as clay and the surviving oak as photograph gets a house style from its own epistemics, and it keeps trust while looking like the references. The open question is whether five hand-built rooms can carry a site of 52 libraries. The companion prototype should answer the visual half of that, and a first measured Long Room chapter on a mid-range phone should answer the performance half before any wider commitment.
