# Rebuild Wanderstacks around real places

**Wanderstacks is wide but fake where it matters: it hides its 3D rooms, shows procedural halls that do not look like the real buildings, and leaves most libraries thin. The fix is to put the reference spine first (library list, book list, search), then a quiet photographic home with motion that never blocks content, then real captures served from the site's own storage.** The audit found the `/room/:lib` route is linked from nowhere, 25 of 52 libraries have no hall data, 278 of 363 hall dimensions are eyeballed from photos, and only 17 readable texts attach to a library. The capture research found that no library offers a clean, free, self-hostable, walkable interior. Only **Weimar (18 equirectangular spheres, CC BY-SA 4.0)** and **Admont (1 sphere, CC BY-SA 4.0)** come close, with Prunksaal (one blurry 16K sphere, CC BY 2.0) and State Library Victoria (a 1.0M-face scan, non-commercial only) as partial cases. Everything else is Commons stills, an exterior sphere or panorama, a link-out tour (Google Arts & Culture, Vatican, ONB), or nothing real. The recommendation is therefore tiered: real stored spheres where they exist, honest "photographs with generated depth" elsewhere, and clearly labelled reconstructions for lost libraries. The street-level sweep (Google, Mapillary, Panoramax; KartaView unchecked) found no confirmed official Street View for any library, real 360 sequences only at **Peabody** (Mapillary and Panoramax) and **BnF** (Panoramax), and mostly isolated user-contributed spheres, so the outside-view tier leans on Commons spheres and the few CC BY-SA street sets, with Google as a click-to-load fallback. About a third of the claims here rest on search snippets, not opened pages; the unverified list near the end names them.

## The current site: a cinematic front over thin, orphaned content

The audit of the `cinematic-site` branch (a Vite/TypeScript SPA, about 7,000 lines in `src`) shows that the last rebuild moved the front door to photographs and left the 3D work stranded. The only reference to `/room/` in `src/` is the route itself, and `src/room/outside.ts` cannot be reached because nothing sets the "outside" arrival state. The home page is a clipped night globe with a large glass panel and a founding-date list; no library imagery appears until a click, and the "To scale" strip renders five empty outline rectangles that read as unfinished (project audit). Public pages leak developer notes such as "Shares the hall bay kit with the Long Room" and "No single title confirmed yet". Two route modules (`stgall`, `darwin`) are five-line stubs.

The "inside" is weaker than it looks. Home "Step inside" for Celsus shows another facade photo labelled interior, although the interior is lost. The 27 procedural rooms use three generic kits (hall, rotunda, site) parameterised by numbers that are mostly estimated: the tally is photo-estimate 278, measured 43, invented 36, excavated 5, attested 1. The Admont room is a brown barrel vault with a checkerboard floor where the real hall is white and gold. The audit concludes this is structural, not a tuning problem: a kit cannot carry a real place's frescoes and colour.

Data depth is the second failure. The audit counts 52 libraries, 202 book records and 269 facts, but 15 to 16 libraries have no facts, only one library has a ledger file (220 bytes), `hours.json` is empty, and verification covers 30 of 52 libraries. The earlier five reports mostly recommended depth on five rooms before breadth; the project did the opposite, listing all 52 libraries and 135 readable titles, largely unrelated Gutenberg novels. The UX note adds that the earlier award-site research optimised for showpiece scroll films and said nothing about catalogue, list or search, which is the owner's second requirement. Treat that as a diagnosis, not a measurement: the UX researcher never saw the live build.

Repository weight is a smaller problem: about 1,820 tracked files and roughly 260 MB of binaries (public/book 126 MB, public/photo 136 MB). Cloudflare Pages allows 25 MiB per file and 20,000 files on the free plan ([Pages limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)), so the file count is safe today but large splats and tile pyramids belong on R2 (10 GB free, free egress, per [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)).

## Motion that wins attention without blocking content

The owner wants "awwwaaards level amazing motion graphics and animations but also useful and informative". The Nielsen Norman Group tested scrolljacking and found "the majority of our study participants were at least mildly disoriented", with task-oriented users the least patient and mobile worse ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). These two demands reconcile if the motion is layered on top of readable content and never stands between the visitor and a link. The test for every effect: the content is in the DOM, readable and reachable with motion switched off, and the effect only changes how it arrives.

Concretely, the home is a normal-scrolling page. The hero is a full-bleed real photograph of one library (chosen daily, which the UX note suggests for return visits), with slow depth parallax or a short ambient loop, a large search field, and three entries: Globe, A-Z, Surprise me. That pattern comes from Google Arts & Culture, whose first screen pairs search with "Click for a surprise" ([Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com)), and from the Rijksmuseum's three-noun navigation ([Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en)). The cinematic film becomes an opt-in "Watch the film" button of 60 to 90 seconds. The owner's "Awwwards level" bar is met by craft inside this frame: typography as a designed object, shared-element transitions from library card to library hero, a globe that flies to a library, and scroll-driven reveals. Getty's "Sculpting Harmony" (Awwwards Site of the Month, November 2023) shows scroll-driven camera work succeeding for one scholarly story ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sculpting-harmony-wins-site-of-the-month-november-2023.html)); the rule is to use that style on a story page with a visible Skip control, never on the first two viewport heights or on catalogue pages.

The platform now carries most of this natively. CSS scroll-driven animations are Baseline Newly available since Firefox 159 in October 2026 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2026)). Cross-document view transitions work in Chrome and Edge 126+ and Safari 18.2+ but not by default in Firefox ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions)), which degrades to a plain navigation. GSAP is free ([gsap.com](https://gsap.com)) and is worth adding only for in-room camera moves. The Rijksmuseum Vermeer site, with UI that fades into the background and a grid of all 37 paintings, averaged 12 minutes per session ([Q42](https://q42.nl/en/work/johannes-vermeer)), which is the evidence that immersive pieces hold people when they are chosen destinations.

Performance and access rules keep the motion honest: LCP 2.5 s, INP 200 ms and CLS 0.1 at the 75th percentile ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)); 24 px minimum targets, a single-pointer alternative to every drag, and focus never hidden under sticky captions ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)); `prefers-reduced-motion` removes parallax and flights but keeps fades. The numeric budgets (about 1 MB of JS and CSS, 1.5 MB of first-view images, no WebGL on home or catalogue) are the UX researcher's own proposals, not sourced.

Navigation follows the same logic: Libraries, Books, Visit, Stories and Search, with Sources and credits in the footer. The globe should be a path, not the hero. MapLibre GL's globe projection with OpenFreeMap tiles is the free, real-map option ([MapLibre clusters](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/create-and-style-clusters/), [OpenFreeMap](https://openfreemap.org/)), with a synchronised list as the accessible equivalent. The existing custom three.js globe can stay if it is reworked so it no longer clips behind a panel. Choosing between them was not tested.

## Real captures: what each library can honestly offer

Ranks: A = real walkable capture, free; B = real 360 stills; C = flat photographs only; D = nothing real. Two researchers covered libraries 1 to 26 and 27 to 52, and a third tested official-tour embeddability. Where they disagree on an A grade, the stricter reading wins here. The licence duties: CC BY needs credit; CC BY-SA needs credit and the same licence on adapted material (a tile pyramid or depth map cut from a sphere is plausibly adapted material, which is a reading of the licence text, not a ruling); CC BY-NC or NC-SA blocks any commercial use of the site; Google Street View, Google Arts & Culture, Vatican and ONB tours give no reuse licence at all.

The street-level column comes from a separate sweep run without a Google or Mapillary key, by driving the public web apps. **G** is the nearest Google sphere the Maps web app snapped to from the library's coordinate (with 70 m offset probes run on only about 20 libraries); **M** is Mapillary coverage within 100 m, a crude pixel count of green map pixels at zoom 19 (under about 200 px is uncertain), with the 360-only filter reported separately; **P** is Panoramax images within a 100 m box. KartaView was not checked (its API timed out). Every Google hit is an isolated user-contributed photo sphere (contributors such as "Pietro" and "Point 360"); none was checked for a connected interior walk, and no official Google Street View was confirmed anywhere. Google "none" is unreliable: State Library Victoria in central Melbourne returned black views, so a "none" does not prove absence. Only Long Room, Celsus, Boston Public Library, Alexandria and SLV had their screenshots viewed; the other Google cells come from the returned URL. Haeinsa is unresolved. Two findings shape the whole plan. First, the owner's wish to avoid third-party hand-offs is mostly satisfiable only for the stored Commons and Sketchfab assets; the official tours the notes found are not embeddable. Google Arts & Culture pages (Prunksaal, Nalanda, Mafra, Klementinum) and the Vatican Sistine Hall tour all sent `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`, and the SLV tour page sends the same plus a frame-ancestors allowlist, so these are link-outs and nothing else. Admont's discover-culture tour, Weimar's 3dtopevent and rooom views, and Trinity's CloudPano tours showed no frame restriction, but the terms of every one were unread, so even those are "ask before embedding", and a hosted Commons sphere is the safer choice for Admont and Weimar. Second, the strongest scan is State Library Victoria's, and it is the one with a non-commercial licence; whether the site counts as non-commercial is an owner decision. Sketchfab's official embed route returned 200 with no frame restriction for Celsus, Mafra, SLV and Palafoxiana ([Sketchfab Celsus](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/library-of-celsus-crowdsourced-photogrammetry-1cc6ad6af508422398f60f902cb70e88)), and downloadable CC models can be converted and self-hosted rather than embedded, which keeps the visitor on the site.

## A pipeline for each content tier

**Tier 1, stored interior spheres (Weimar, Admont, Prunksaal; SLV if non-commercial).** Download the Commons originals, cut them into a tiled pyramid with a low-resolution base, and show them in Photo Sphere Viewer v5 (MIT, three.js, with Virtual Tour, Markers, Map and Compass plugins; [GitHub](https://github.com/mistic100/Photo-Sphere-Viewer), [Virtual Tour docs](https://photo-sphere-viewer.js.org/plugins/virtual-tour.html)). Marzipano is archived as of 2026-04-20 and should not be picked ([Marzipano](https://github.com/google/marzipano)). Weimar's 18 spheres become a real hopping tour; movement between nodes is a crossfade with the camera turning toward the link, optionally upgraded to a depth-warped transition using DA360 panorama depth ([arXiv 2512.22819](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22819)), whose licence was not found and which would be custom three.js work. Admont and Prunksaal are single spheres, shown as "one real panorama", with no invented rooms in between. The SLV scan, if used, is converted from Sketchfab download to a compressed GLB or splat and walked with a waypoint graph, the approach PlayCanvas used for Grace Cathedral (about 3.5M splats on desktop, 1.4M on mobile; [PlayCanvas blog](https://blog.playcanvas.com/building-the-grace-cathedral-experience)). Credit line, licence and "N real photographs" label sit in an info panel.

**Tier 2, real outside (exterior sphere, wide panorama, 3D exterior).** Vasconcelos, Alexandrina, Celsus, Palafoxiana, Vikramashila, Dunhuang, NLI, Sassoon and Ashikaga have a usable exterior asset. Spheres go into the same viewer; wide partial panoramas map onto a cylinder or partial sphere behind the library name, shown honestly as a wide photograph. For libraries with a Panoramax, Mapillary or KartaView 360 near the entrance, stored images under CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution are legal to re-serve ([OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Street-level_imagery)); Mapillary's own terms on bulk storage were not read, so check them before mirroring. The sweep assigns this branch as follows. **Peabody** is the strongest self-hostable exterior candidate, with 360 coverage on both Mapillary (102 px of 360) and Panoramax (54 images, 48 of them 360, CC BY-SA 4.0 per the earlier inventory). **BnF** has Panoramax 360 sets (200+ images in the box, 157 360) but no Mapillary. Klementinum (Mapillary 360, 4227 px), Stockholm, Morgan and Sainte-Geneviève have Mapillary 360 coverage, and Long Room's Mapillary imagery is entirely 360. Prunksaal, Palafoxiana, Buda, Ottawa, Admont and Joanina have Mapillary flat imagery plus a Google sphere within about 10 m, which supports a flat-photo or click-to-load outside view, not a stored sphere. Gabinete, Wiblingen, Nineveh, Timbuktu, Vikramashila and Malatestiana returned nothing on any service, and Haeinsa is unresolved; for the three urban ones (Gabinete, Wiblingen, Malatestiana) the empty result more likely reflects the Maps snap limit than true absence. These are presence signals, not checked captures: no stored image was inspected for quality, and Mapillary's own terms on bulk storage were not read, so check them before mirroring. Google Street View is the fallback only as a live Maps Embed iframe, which is free and unlimited but must be requested dynamically and cannot be cached or merged with anything stored ([Embed usage](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/embed/usage-and-billing), [Maps service terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-service-terms), summary only). It is a third-party frame, so load it only on a visitor click ("Look around, powered by Google"), never by default. A neutral surroundings model of OSM and Overture buildings on OpenFreeMap tiles is storable under ODbL with attribution ([Overture](https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/)). Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles and Cesium ion Community are not viable (1,000 events a month, no offline use, non-commercial; [Google tiles policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies), [Cesium pricing](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/pricing/)).

**Tier 3, stills only (about 30 libraries).** Do not fake a walk. Build a gallery of the best Commons photographs with credit, an interior-and-exterior pairing, and, for hero photos only, a gentle depth effect from a single-image splat. Apple SHARP runs on Apple Silicon and outputs standard Gaussian PLY in under a second on a GPU, but its model licence was not read ([ml-sharp](https://github.com/apple/ml-sharp)). Label these "photograph with generated depth". Do not outpaint a photo to a full sphere unless the label says imagined, and never generate inscriptions, book spines or faces. Reconstructing a room from Commons photos with COLMAP and Brush is unproven: the research expects 5 to 30 overlapping images per room to register poorly and recommends trying it on one or two best-covered rooms only. MASt3R and the original VGGT weights are non-commercial; VGGT-1B-Commercial needs an application form ([VGGT](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vggt)). If any splat is made, store it as SOG or SPZ, which PlayCanvas rates at 10 to 20 times smaller than PLY ([formats](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/gaussian-splatting/formats/)), and render it with Spark (MIT, three.js; [Spark docs](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/)), which fits the three.js stack the repo already has.

**Tier 4, lost or unrecorded libraries (Alexandria, Nineveh, Baghdad, Buda, Herculaneum, Pergamon, parts of Nalanda).** The existing hall kit survives here, but only with sourced dimensions and a permanent "reconstruction, imagined" label in the HUD, not a footnote. The London Charter and Seville Principles call for transparent paradata ([London Charter](https://londoncharter.org/introduction.html)). Show the real ruin or the holder of the surviving objects beside it: British Museum and CDLI for Nineveh, the ruin panoramas for Vikramashila and Nalanda. Dunhuang's Library Cave twin is a closed Tencent build ([Tencent](https://www.tencent.com/en-us/articles/2201835.html)) and stays a link.

## Books, search and lists as the site's spine

The data layer is already close. `books.json` has shelfmark, catalogue URL, a `read` block with licence and CORS flag, source, quote and a check command. What is missing is scope and discipline. The recommended build harvests open metadata at build time into a committed `catalogue.ndjson` (Wikidata CC0, where **307,086 items carry an IIIF manifest URL**, queried live in the notes; Europeana CC0 metadata; Open Library dumps; the 5.8 MB Gutenberg catalogue CSV) and fetches IIIF manifests at runtime only from hosts that send CORS headers. Live tests showed `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on Bodleian, e-codices and Yale manifests ([e-codices Plan of St Gall](https://www.e-codices.unifr.ch/metadata/iiif/csg-1092/manifest.json), [Yale Voynich](https://collections.library.yale.edu/manifests/2002046)), and none on the Vatican manifest ([DigiVatLib](https://digi.vatlib.it/iiif/MSS_Vat.lat.3225/manifest.json)). Library of Congress, Gallica and DPLA returned 403 to the notes' scripted requests, so they are harvested by key or by hand, never at runtime.

Rights are the gate, and the catalogue should carry three states: `open` (CC0, public domain mark, CC BY), `nc` (e-codices is CC BY-NC 4.0, so it needs the non-commercial question answered), and `link-only` (the Book of Kells needs written permission from Trinity; Vatican images are marked copyright; the British Library offers no downloads after its cyberattack). A nightly script should fetch each manifest, record canvases, licence and thumbnail, and set the binary passing or failing status, which also closes the 19 records whose canvas count is null. The reader choice follows the item: OpenSeadragon for single deep-zoom images, Mirador or Universal Viewer for codices, and the existing page-flip over Image API URLs when the product wants its own look.

For search, Pagefind is recommended at both 5,000 and 50,000 records. It builds a chunked static index, supports filters and sorts, and accepts non-HTML records through `addCustomRecord` ([Pagefind docs](https://pagefind.app/docs/), [Node API](https://pagefind.app/docs/node-api/)). Each book gets a static page `/books/{id}` so it is linkable and indexable. Facets: library, region, century, language, subject, access. Following Baymard, applied filters show as removable chips with Clear all ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/how-to-design-applied-filters)). The sizing figures (about 3 MB raw for 5,000 records) are the researcher's estimate, and no index was benchmarked, so a 20-minute spike on a synthetic 50k file comes before committing. The current Cmd+K palette (230 entries, substring match on title and subtitle) and the `/shelf` filter box get replaced by one search box returning typed results (Library, Book, Author).

The book list needs to be about libraries, not novels. The audit found only 17 readable texts attached to a library, covering 5 libraries, and 19 libraries with zero or one book. The notes propose 10 curated treasures per library (about 520) plus roughly 5,000 catalogue records at launch, each treasure carrying shelfmark, date range, language, provenance, digitised block with rights, a human-written "why", and a citation. The books note adds `claim_type` (fact, superlative, estimate) because superlatives like "first civic library" (Malatestiana) and "largest chained library" (Hereford) rest on Wikipedia and need primary sources.

The library list is also incomplete in a particular way: the 52 skew toward Central European Baroque halls and Anglophone reading rooms. The notes propose about 30 additions, with this priority ten: Laurenziana, Ambrosiana, St Catherine's Monastery, Süleymaniye, Matenadaran, Kyujanggak, Beinecke, Wren Library, National Library of Brazil and Al-Qarawiyyin. Several of these rest on background knowledge only, and the Al-Qarawiyyin, Astan Quds Razavi, Dar al-Kutub and Kaiser Library Wikipedia pages returned 404 under the titles tried. Treat the list as candidates until each gets a targeted source check. Do not add libraries before the 52 are deep; the notes' main lesson from the earlier reports is that breadth outran depth.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we build a better 3D room" to "how little invention can each library honestly carry". For most of the 52 the answer is a handful of credited photographs, a few for which a real sphere or scan exists, and a labelled reconstruction for the rest. The strongest assets, Weimar's 18 spheres and Admont's sphere, are exactly the ones a Photo Sphere Viewer tour can host without leaving the site, so the first visible win comes from data work rather than rendering work.

Two owner decisions gate the rest: whether the site counts as non-commercial (it unlocks the SLV scan and e-codices) and whether a click-to-load Google Street View frame is acceptable as a fallback. Everything else follows from putting the catalogue before the cinema and letting the motion decorate content that is already there.
